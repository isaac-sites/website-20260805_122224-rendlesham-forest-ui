## Introduction

The three shallow ground marks found in a clearing at Rendlesham Forest have long been presented as the strongest physical indication that an unidentified craft landed near RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }}). Yet the evidential value of those impressions depends on a critical assumption: that they formed a coherent landing-gear pattern. Contemporary police observations did not reach that conclusion. Instead, officers recorded three shallow impressions but found no convincing mechanical arrangement, and at least one officer suggested they could have been made by an animal. Later supporters of the UFO interpretation linked the marks into a triangular configuration, but that interpretation remains disputed because no accompanying physical evidence typical of a landed vehicle was documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold FileMay 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2026</span></span></span>

## What the daylight inspection actually recorded

The impressions were examined after daylight, following the overnight reports of unusual lights in the forest. Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s later memorandum described three depressions approximately 1.5 inches (3.8 cm) deep and about 7 inches (18 cm) across. These dimensions became one of the best-known physical details associated with the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold FileMay 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2026</span></span></span>

However, the police inspection served a different purpose from Halt's later summary. Officers were not attempting to prove or disprove an extraordinary event; they were checking whether anything had actually landed. According to the surviving police account, they found three shallow impressions but did not identify them as the footprint of an aircraft or other machine. Their observations stopped short of confirming that the marks formed a deliberate geometric landing pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-rendlesham-forest-britains-roswell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: Rendlesham Forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: Rendlesham Forest - Britain’s Roswell? - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. The existence of three depressions is not itself disputed. The disagreement concerns what those depressions signify.

## Why three points do not establish a landing pattern

Three points can always define a triangle in geometry, but that alone does not demonstrate a mechanical structure.

For investigators examining a suspected landing site, the crucial questions would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether the spacing between the impressions matched a stable support geometry;
* whether the impressions had comparable depth, shape and loading characteristics;
* whether they displayed signs of weight compression rather than simple disturbance;
* whether they aligned with any other physical traces expected from a landed object.

</div>

The available [contemporary records]({{ 'contemporary-record/' | relative_url }}) do not document such measurements. Instead, later descriptions generally refer only to three shallow depressions without providing surveyed distances, precise geometry or engineering analysis capable of demonstrating that they functioned as landing-gear footprints.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold FileMay 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2026</span></span></span>

As a result, the argument that a craft rested on three legs relies largely on interpretation rather than on a documented forensic reconstruction.

## The police and forestry interpretation

Independent civilian observers reached noticeably more mundane conclusions than many later UFO accounts.

Police officers reportedly considered that the impressions might have been produced by animals rather than machinery. Local Forestry Commission personnel, including forester Vince Thurkettle, later identified the depressions as rabbit diggings or similar natural disturbances common within the forest. Other supposed supporting features, such as marks on nearby trees, were likewise given ordinary explanations by forestry workers rather than being treated as evidence of a powerful landing event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theblackarchive.net/en/case/011" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackarchive.net">[검은 기록 보관소]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackarchive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">검은 기록 보관소The Rendlesham Forest Incident — CASE FILE 011 &#124; The Black Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">검은 기록 보관소The Rendlesham Forest Incident — CASE FILE 011 &#124; The Black Archive</span></span></span>

These explanations do not prove that every aspect of the wider Rendlesham incident has a conventional cause. They do, however, show that the ground marks themselves failed to persuade the independent people who inspected the site in its normal environmental context.

## What stronger landing evidence would normally include

If a substantial mechanical object had briefly settled in a forest clearing, investigators would generally expect more than three isolated depressions.

Depending on the object's design and weight, stronger supporting evidence might include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* consistent pressure marks showing significant loading of the soil;
* displaced earth matching the shape of landing pads or supports;
* repeated or symmetrical impressions with measurable spacing;
* scraping, sliding or rotation marks from touchdown or departure;
* debris, residue or transferred material;
* wider disturbance to surrounding vegetation caused by weight or exhaust.

</div>

None of these features were convincingly documented by the police investigation. Later accounts introduced [broken branches]({{ 'broken-branches/' | relative_url }}), tree abrasions and [radiation readings]({{ 'radiation/' | relative_url }}) into the broader discussion, but these remain separate evidential questions. The ground impressions alone did not demonstrate the distinctive physical signature expected from a heavy landed vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold FileMay 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why the three marks became so influential

The impressions acquired much greater significance after they were combined with witness testimony describing a triangular craft.

Viewed in isolation, three shallow depressions are ambiguous. Viewed alongside reports of an object allegedly resting on three supports, they appear more suggestive. This illustrates a common problem in evaluating physical evidence: witness accounts can influence how otherwise uncertain traces are interpreted.

Because the impressions lacked distinctive mechanical characteristics, both interpretations remained possible. Supporters regarded them as [corroboration]({{ 'corroboration/' | relative_url }}) of eyewitness testimony, while sceptical investigators argued that the witness accounts encouraged observers to see an organised landing pattern where the physical evidence alone did not establish one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold FileMay 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 2026</span></span></span>

## What the ground marks can—and cannot—show

The daylight inspection established that three shallow impressions existed in the clearing. It did not establish that an aircraft or unidentified craft had landed there.

That distinction remains one of the most important evidential boundaries in the Rendlesham Forest case. The impressions are genuine observations recorded soon after the event, but they are not self-interpreting. Without measurements demonstrating a coherent landing geometry, associated mechanical traces, or recovered physical material, the marks fall short of confirming a landing. The [police response]({{ 'police-response/' | relative_url }}) therefore provides a useful counterweight to later narratives: while the three depressions became central to claims of a landed craft, the independent officers who inspected them did not conclude that they formed the footprint of one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-rendlesham-forest-britains-roswell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: Rendlesham Forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: Rendlesham Forest - Britain’s Roswell? - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>
