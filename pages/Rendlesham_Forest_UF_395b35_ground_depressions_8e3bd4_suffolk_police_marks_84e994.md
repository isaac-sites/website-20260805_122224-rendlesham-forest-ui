## Introduction

One of the central debates in the Rendlesham Forest incident is whether three shallow [ground depressions]({{ 'ground-marks/' | relative_url }}) represented the landing points of an unidentified craft. Suffolk police, however, did not regard the marks as persuasive physical evidence. Officers who inspected the clearing soon after the first reported sighting concluded that the impressions were unremarkable and could have been caused by animals rather than by a landed vehicle. Their restrained assessment stands in marked contrast to later accounts that treated the depressions as one of the strongest pieces of evidence for a UFO landing. Contemporary police observations matter because they were made before the story had developed through years of retelling and before the ground marks acquired their later symbolic importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## What officers observed at the clearing

Suffolk Constabulary became involved twice on the morning after the initial reports. Officers were first asked to investigate lights seen in the forest. They reported seeing no unusual object and identified the visible flashing light as the Orford Ness [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}). Later that morning they returned specifically to inspect the reported ground impressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

When police examined the clearing, they found only three small impressions in the soil. Rather than interpreting them as evidence of landing gear, officers considered them ordinary disturbances that could readily have been produced by wildlife. This judgement was recorded long before the site became internationally famous and before detailed UFO narratives had become established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

Independent accounts from later researchers who interviewed or documented the early witnesses are consistent on this point. Ian Ridpath's reconstruction, based on [police records]({{ 'police-record/' | relative_url }}) and later testimony, reports that the officers regarded the impressions as animal-made and found nothing to support the idea that a craft had landed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## What evidence the police did not find

The significance of the police assessment lies as much in what was absent as in what was present. Officers did not report:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* deep, sharply defined landing impressions;
* crushed vegetation consistent with a heavy object resting on the ground;
* scorched or fused soil;
* debris or fragments;
* fluid residues or unusual materials;
* any hazard requiring the site to be treated as the scene of an aircraft accident.

</div>

Instead, the marks appeared to them to be minor surface disturbances with no distinctive mechanical characteristics. Nothing at the scene compelled the conclusion that a vehicle—conventional or otherwise—had touched down.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

This is particularly noteworthy because the initial response had begun as a possible aircraft-crash investigation. Had officers encountered convincing physical evidence of a recent landing, it would have been expected to feature prominently in police reporting. No such evidence emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-rendlesham-forest-britains-roswell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: Rendlesham Forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: Rendlesham Forest - Britain’s Roswell? - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

## Why the animal explanation appeared plausible

The police interpretation was reinforced by people familiar with the forest itself. Forestry Commission forester Vince Thurkettle, who later viewed the location, identified the depressions as [rabbit scrapes]({{ 'rabbit-scrapes/' | relative_url }}) rather than engineered impressions. He recalled that they were irregular, appeared old, and were partly covered with fallen pine needles rather than looking freshly created.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

This explanation also addressed features that later became associated with the alleged landing site. Marks described as "burns" on nearby trees were identified by Thurkettle as routine forestry axe marks used to indicate trees scheduled for felling, while [broken branches]({{ 'broken-branches/' | relative_url }}) were considered ordinary features of a managed woodland. These observations reduced the need to invoke a single dramatic event to explain multiple features of the clearing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

Importantly, neither the police nor the forester denied that the depressions existed. Their disagreement concerned interpretation. They regarded the marks as commonplace rather than diagnostic.

## Why the timing of the police inspection matters

The evidential value of the police assessment comes largely from its timing. Officers inspected the site within hours of the reported events, before weather, visitors or prolonged discussion could substantially alter the scene. Their observations therefore represent one of the earliest independent examinations of the alleged landing area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-rendlesham-forest-britains-roswell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: Rendlesham Forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: Rendlesham Forest - Britain’s Roswell? - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

## Why police and military interpretations diverged

The differing conclusions arose partly because the two groups approached the site with different questions.

Military personnel investigating an already unusual sequence of reported lights naturally considered whether the depressions might fit the reported object's location. Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s later memorandum described three depressions about seven inches across and one and a half inches deep arranged in a triangular pattern, a description that encouraged later interpretations involving landing gear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-uk-rendlesham-defe-24-1948-defe-24-1948-rendlesham-forest-file" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRendlesham Forest Incident, DEFE 24/1948 (Halt Memo, Suffolk Constabulary correspondence, parliamentary briefings), UK Mi...</span></span></span>

Police officers, however, evaluated the ground as a potential physical scene rather than as [corroboration]({{ 'corroboration/' | relative_url }}) of witness testimony. Their conclusion reflected the normal evidential standard used in scene examination: unusual claims require corresponding physical evidence, and the clearing did not provide it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## What the police assessment means for the landing-mark claim

The Suffolk police view does not resolve every aspect of the Rendlesham Forest incident, nor does it explain every reported light in the sky. Its significance is narrower but important: it weakens the specific claim that the three depressions constituted convincing physical proof of a landed craft.

The early inspection found only minor ground disturbances that officers considered insignificant and consistent with ordinary natural causes. Because this assessment was made promptly, independently and before decades of retelling transformed the depressions into a central feature of the case, it remains one of the strongest contemporary pieces of evidence against interpreting the marks themselves as proof of a UFO landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>
