## Introduction

John Burroughs was one of the United States Air Force security personnel who entered Rendlesham Forest after unusual lights were reported beyond RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }})’s East Gate in the early hours of 26 December 1980. His experience matters because his written statement, produced within days of the patrol, is far less elaborate than many later versions of the incident. It records coloured lights, difficult terrain, alarming noises and a pursuit that ultimately carried the men towards a distant farmhouse and [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}). It does not clearly describe Burroughs examining a landed spacecraft.

That gap is central to understanding the case. Burroughs plainly believed that the patrol encountered something extraordinary, and he continued to say so decades later. Yet the earliest surviving account leaves the nature, distance and even physical form of what he saw uncertain. ## His role in the patrol

Burroughs was serving with the 81st Security Police Squadron at RAF Woodbridge, one of the adjoining British bases then operated by the US Air Force. Security personnel near the East Gate saw red and blue lights beyond the perimeter and initially treated them as a possible aircraft emergency. This was not an unreasonable reaction: the forest began close to the end of the runway, and a descending light could have suggested an aircraft had crashed or made a forced landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Accounts differ slightly over the patrol’s precise composition and sequence. In the fuller later reconstruction, Burroughs and Staff Sergeant Bud Steffens first drove towards the lights, returned to the gate and requested assistance. Staff Sergeant Jim Penniston then arrived with Airman Edward [Cabansag]({{ 'cabansag/' | relative_url }}). Steffens remained near the base while Burroughs, Penniston and Cabansag proceeded into the forest, first by vehicle and then on foot when the track became unsuitable. Penniston, as the senior man in the group, assumed control of the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

The patrol was reportedly unarmed once it moved beyond the base boundary. A surviving Suffolk police record describes RAF Woodbridge as having sent “unarmed troops” to investigate unusual lights, which the base was provisionally calling a UFO. That wording is important: “UFO” was being used as a description of something unidentified, not as an official conclusion that the men were pursuing an extraterrestrial craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/suffolk-constabulary-have-record-dated.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">suffolk constabulary have record dated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">at present&quot;.# The Rendlesham Forest Incident Official Blog... ### Suffolk Constabulary have a record, dated 26 December 1980, of a repor...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1980</span></span></span>

Burroughs’s position in the patrol gave him a different perspective from Penniston. Penniston later portrayed himself as the investigator who approached, inspected and touched a triangular vehicle. Burroughs did not subsequently recall that sequence in the same way. He remembered a brilliant light, going to the ground and seeing an intense red or sun-like source, but not standing beside Penniston while a solid craft was examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

## What the early statement actually describes

Burroughs’s written statement is one of five surviving accounts associated with the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}). It is valuable because it was recorded close to the event, before books, documentaries, hypnosis sessions and decades of public debate had reshaped the story. It should not be treated as a perfect transcript of events, but it carries more evidential weight than recollections first made public many years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2Here are the original statements provided by five of the participants … namely...</span></span></span>

His account begins with lights in the forest rather than a clearly visible vehicle. He described red and blue flashing lights, accompanied by an intense white light. As the patrol advanced, the lights seemed to be near a farmer’s house beyond the trees. The men crossed difficult ground and fences while trying to determine their origin. Burroughs also reported that nearby farm animals became noisy and agitated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span></span></span>

At one stage, Burroughs and Penniston went prone. In the early account, this action was connected to movement and disturbing sounds in the forest rather than to the take-off of a spacecraft. One noise resembled a woman screaming, a sound later associated with the call of a muntjac deer. The episode illustrates how the patrol’s surroundings could intensify uncertainty: darkness, unfamiliar animal cries, intermittent lights and broken sightlines through trees made distance and direction difficult to judge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Burroughs reported that the men eventually reached the edge of the forest and saw the lights towards a farmhouse. From there, at least one recurring light was recognised as a lighthouse or lighthouse-like beacon farther away. Cabansag’s account likewise described the pursued light as a distant beacon. This does not prove that every light seen earlier was the Orfordness lighthouse, but it establishes that a lighthouse entered the witnesses’ own interpretation during the patrol rather than being invented solely by later sceptics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span></span></span>

The statement also included a drawing showing a dark central form or area with red, blue and white lights around it. Supporters regard this as evidence that Burroughs saw a structured object. The difficulty is that the accompanying prose does not unambiguously identify it as a solid machine, give reliable dimensions or describe Burroughs approaching its surface. The sketch preserves his visual impression, but it cannot by itself settle whether he drew a physical craft, an arrangement of lights or the way a bright source appeared through the trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

## The patrol’s experience and the lighthouse question

The conventional explanation does not require Burroughs to have fabricated anything. It proposes that the initial sighting may have been prompted by a bright [fireball]({{ 'fireball/' | relative_url }}), after which the men pursued intermittent terrestrial lights through a confusing landscape. From parts of their route, the Orfordness lighthouse lay broadly beyond the forest and near the line of sight towards the farmhouse. Its beam could appear, disappear and change apparent brightness as trees, ground contours and observers’ movement alternately obscured it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span></span></span>

This explanation fits several features of Burroughs’s early statement: the lights seemed to move as the patrol moved; they proved farther away than first assumed; and the men eventually described a light in the direction of the farmhouse and lighthouse. Suffolk police who attended later reported finding no crashed aircraft or unexplained object and identified the principal visible light in the area as the Orford lighthouse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/suffolk-constabulary-have-record-dated.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">suffolk constabulary have record dated</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">at present&quot;.# The Rendlesham Forest Incident Official Blog... ### Suffolk Constabulary have a record, dated 26 December 1980, of a repor...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1980</span></span></span>

It does not neatly explain every impression. Burroughs consistently described more than a single white flash: he remembered red and blue lights, a powerful white illumination and an experience intense enough to make trained security personnel continue into the forest. A lighthouse explanation is strongest for the persistent flashing light seen at distance; it is less decisive when applied indiscriminately to every colour, movement and close-range impression reported during the patrol.

Distance estimation is the key uncertainty. In darkness, a bright point with no visible background can appear nearby, particularly when viewed through trees. When an observer advances but cannot reach it, the light may seem to retreat or move. The patrol’s own route—from the base perimeter, through the forest and towards open fields—suggests that at least part of the apparent encounter involved men closing on a source that remained substantially farther away than they initially believed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2d.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span></span></span>

## Where later memories diverged

The best-known version of the first night is largely Penniston’s later account. He eventually described a black triangular craft resting in a clearing, supported by beams of light. He said he walked around it, touched its surface, examined engraved symbols and experienced a stream of binary numbers. These claims transformed the episode from an investigation of unusual lights into an alleged close encounter with technology of unknown origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Burroughs did not independently confirm that complete sequence. His later recollections included an exceptionally bright light and a red, oval or sun-like form, followed by limited memory of what happened during the crucial interval. Penniston, by contrast, said Burroughs stood motionless while the craft was present and appeared not to respond when called. Penniston interpreted this as evidence that Burroughs was somehow incapacitated or affected by the phenomenon. Burroughs’s lack of matching recall means the claim rests primarily on Penniston’s testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

The timing of the details matters. Neither Burroughs’s nor Penniston’s publicly available early statement contains the later story in full. Burroughs did not report seeing Penniston touch a hull, and Penniston’s early account did not mention the binary message, extended physical inspection or prolonged missing interval. A triangular or metallic object was nevertheless described in Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt’s January 1981 memorandum, showing that some form of structured-object account was circulating at the base within weeks. Halt’s memo, however, was a second-hand summary of the first patrol rather than a statement written by Burroughs himself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Supporters argue that the early statements may have been constrained or altered under military pressure. Penniston later claimed that superiors replaced a longer report with an approved version, while Cabansag said that he signed his statement under duress. These allegations are difficult to verify independently. Indeed, Penniston has also said that he believed Burroughs’s statement was the one early account that had not been tampered with, which makes its failure to describe the later close encounter especially significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Memory does not have to be dishonest to change. Repeated retelling, discussion with fellow witnesses, hypnosis, media reconstructions and exposure to other accounts can alter how an event is organised and understood. Burroughs’s caution about what he personally remembered distinguishes his testimony from Penniston’s increasingly detailed narrative. It also prevents the two men’s accounts from being merged as though they were a single, mutually confirmed story.

## The later medical claim

Burroughs later developed serious health problems and sought assistance from the United States Department of Veterans Affairs. He argued that his illnesses were connected to exposure during the Rendlesham encounter and said that relevant service or medical records had been restricted. With help from the office of Senator John McCain, his case was eventually resolved and the Department of Veterans Affairs agreed to cover treatment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

The outcome is often presented as official confirmation that Burroughs was injured by a UFO or by exotic radiation. That interpretation goes beyond the publicly available evidence. The detailed medical and administrative basis for the decision has not been released in a form that allows independent assessment, and a benefits award or settlement does not necessarily establish the precise physical cause alleged by a claimant. The available reporting confirms that Burroughs received support; it does not publicly demonstrate that an unknown craft produced the illness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

Burroughs’s case referred in part to the Ministry of Defence study commonly known as Project Condign, which considered whether unusual atmospheric phenomena might produce electromagnetic effects capable of affecting observers. Such material offers a possible mechanism in broad terms, but it is not a contemporaneous medical diagnosis of Burroughs and does not identify what he encountered in the forest. Meanwhile, the radiation readings recorded during the later Halt investigation have been disputed, with critics arguing that they were close to normal background levels and were taken with equipment not designed for the fine environmental comparison later attributed to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on theguardian.com.</span></span></span>

A 2015 Ministry of Defence Freedom of Information response confirmed that Rendlesham-related material existed in government records and directed the requester towards files already transferred or being prepared for The National [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}). It did not disclose information proving the nature of Burroughs’s medical exposure or endorse his interpretation of the patrol.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f599240f0b6230268ef6d/20150511-FOI2015-03810-Rendlesham-Redacted-Final-Response.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">assets.publishing.service.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20150511 FOI2015 03810 Rendlesham Redacted Final Response</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">20150511 FOI2015 03810 Rendlesham Redacted Final Response</span></span></span>

## What Burroughs’s evidence can support

Burroughs’s testimony strongly supports several limited conclusions. A security patrol genuinely responded to unusual lights beyond RAF Woodbridge. The men entered Rendlesham Forest expecting a possible aircraft emergency. They encountered confusing lights and sounds, crossed substantial ground and returned without finding an ordinary wreck. Burroughs found the experience sufficiently extraordinary to maintain for decades that something serious had happened to him.

His evidence is much weaker as proof of a landed vehicle. The earliest statement does not clearly say that he saw or touched a solid craft. It places at least one important light towards a farmhouse and lighthouse, and it records circumstances in which distance, colour and movement could easily be misjudged. The later account of a close inspection comes predominantly from Penniston and is not independently reproduced in Burroughs’s memory.

That distinction does not reduce Burroughs to a witness who merely “saw a lighthouse”. His patrol may have reacted to more than one visual stimulus, beginning with a dramatic transient light and continuing with distant beacons, stars, reflections or other sources seen under poor conditions. The enduring mystery lies less in whether the airmen were frightened or sincere than in whether their initial sensory experience justifies the physical story subsequently built around it.

For reconstructing the first night, Burroughs is therefore most useful when his testimony is read in layers. His early statement documents an intense but ambiguous pursuit of lights. His later recollections add personal consequences, memory gaps and a conviction that the event was not adequately explained. Penniston’s later narrative supplies the detailed craft encounter, but Burroughs does not provide the independent confirmation that would be needed to treat that narrative as established fact.
