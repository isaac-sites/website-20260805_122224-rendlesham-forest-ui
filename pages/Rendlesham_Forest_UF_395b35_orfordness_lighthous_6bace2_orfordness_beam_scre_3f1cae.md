## Introduction

One of the most persistent objections to the Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}) explanation for the Rendlesham Forest incident is that the lighthouse's beam was supposedly screened on its western side and therefore could not have illuminated the forest. The available technical evidence and later field investigations do not support such a simple conclusion. The screening was designed to shape the lighthouse's navigational sectors rather than create a perfect blackout behind the tower, and photographs taken while the lighthouse was still operational show that flashes remained visible from parts of the forest and its eastern edge. The key question is therefore not whether the lighthouse had screening—it did—but whether that screening completely prevented the rotating beam from being seen from Rendlesham. The evidence indicates that it did not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritage.suffolk.gov.uk/Monument/MSF22526" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritage.suffolk.gov.uk">[Suffolk Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritage.suffolk.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Suffolk Heritage ORF 112</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Suffolk HeritageORF 112 - Orford Ness Lighthouse - Suffolk Heritage ExplorerJune 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the shielding was designed to do

Like many major coastal lights, Orfordness Lighthouse incorporated sector lighting and screening to alter the appearance of the light for mariners approaching from different bearings. Historical descriptions of the lighthouse show separate red and green sector lights in addition to the main rotating optic, reflecting its role as a navigational aid rather than a simple omnidirectional beacon. The revolving apparatus itself consisted of three large Fresnel lenses producing a white flash every [five seconds]({{ 'five-seconds/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritage.suffolk.gov.uk/Monument/MSF22526" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritage.suffolk.gov.uk">[Suffolk Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritage.suffolk.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Suffolk Heritage ORF 112</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Suffolk HeritageORF 112 - Orford Ness Lighthouse - Suffolk Heritage ExplorerJune 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2024</span></span></span>

Critics sometimes interpret the presence of a rear screen as meaning that the western hemisphere received no light whatsoever. However, lighthouse screening normally controls the operational sector of [the beam]({{ 'the-beam/' | relative_url }}) rather than guaranteeing absolute optical isolation. The structure was intended to prevent misleading signals to shipping, not to eliminate every possible line of sight through glazing, around structural edges or through the rotating optical assembly. Consequently, the mere existence of screening does not demonstrate that an observer inland could never glimpse the flashes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://heritage.suffolk.gov.uk/Monument/MSF22526" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: heritage.suffolk.gov.uk">[Suffolk Heritage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">heritage.suffolk.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Suffolk Heritage ORF 112</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Suffolk HeritageORF 112 - Orford Ness Lighthouse - Suffolk Heritage ExplorerJune 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 20, 2024</span></span></span>

## Notches and gaps in the screened panes

The strongest evidence against the "completely blocked" claim comes from observations made before the lighthouse was decommissioned.

Astronomy writer Ian Ridpath revisited the area repeatedly over several decades. During later surveys he found that the top of the lighthouse became visible only from specific positions where distant gaps in the tree line aligned with the tower. Super-telephoto photographs taken from the eastern edge of Rendlesham Forest show the lantern room appearing through one such notch between trees. Additional photographs taken at the lighthouse itself from the reciprocal viewing direction demonstrated that the same alignment intersected the lantern windows containing the rotating Fresnel lenses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/rendlesham-forest-ufo-incident.13457/page-9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest UFO Incident &#124; Page 9 &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest UFO Incident &#124; Page 9 &#124; MetabunkMay 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2, 2024</span></span></span>

These photographs are significant because they address the precise criticism that screening should have hidden the beam. If the screened side had been completely opaque across the relevant bearing, the periodic flashes would not have been visible from the documented observation points. Instead, photographs and video recordings captured the flashes themselves, indicating that the optical geometry allowed at least part of the rotating light to reach those positions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span></span></span>

This does not imply that the lighthouse was visible everywhere within Rendlesham Forest. On the contrary, [visibility]({{ 'visibility/' | relative_url }}) appears to have been highly dependent on position. Ridpath noted that only a limited area provided a direct line of sight through the distant trees, helping explain why different witnesses reported different views even while looking generally eastwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/rendlesham-forest-ufo-incident.13457/page-9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest UFO Incident &#124; Page 9 &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest UFO Incident &#124; Page 9 &#124; MetabunkMay 2, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2, 2024</span></span></span>

## Visibility tests before the light was extinguished

Because Orfordness Lighthouse remained operational until 2013, investigators were able to test the hypothesis directly rather than relying solely on maps or recollections.

Television filming during Ridpath's early investigations recorded the rotating flashes from within the forest. He reported that the beam appeared low above the ground because the forest stood slightly higher than the coastline, creating the impression of a nearby moving light among the trees despite its actual distance. The flashes also appeared to shift relative to foreground trees as observers changed position, an expected effect of viewing a distant source through woodland.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span></span></span>

Later photographic surveys before demolition continued to document the same phenomenon. Although the lighthouse itself was often difficult to identify in daylight because of distance and intervening vegetation, the flash remained detectable from favourable viewpoints. ## Why the screening objection remains disputed

The debate persists partly because different investigators have observed the site from different locations.

Supporters of the screening objection point out that from many positions inside the forest the lighthouse cannot be seen directly, arguing that the rear shield and intervening woodland would have prevented the beam from explaining the reported sightings. Modern critics have also published photographs intended to show how limited the visible area was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rendleshamufo.blogspot.com/p/the-lighthouse_4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rendleshamufo.blogspot.com">[rendleshamufo.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rendleshamufo.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Rendlesham Forest Incident: The lighthouse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest Incident: The lighthouse</span></span></span>

However, this argument is stronger against an exaggerated version of the lighthouse theory than against the more limited explanation. The question is not whether the lighthouse was plainly visible throughout the forest but whether flashes from the lantern could reach the locations where key observations were made. Contemporary photographs, reciprocal sight-line studies and filmed demonstrations indicate that they could under at least some viewing geometries. That finding is consistent with the fact that witnesses described intermittent flashes rather than a continuously visible lighthouse structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span></span></span>
