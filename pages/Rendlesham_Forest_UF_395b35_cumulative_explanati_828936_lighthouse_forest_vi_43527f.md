## Introduction

A key question in the conventional explanation of the Rendlesham Forest incident is whether [the beam]({{ 'the-beam/' | relative_url }}) from Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}) could realistically have been seen from within the forest at all. If the answer were clearly "no", the lighthouse hypothesis would largely collapse. If the answer is "yes, but only intermittently", it becomes easier to understand why witnesses described a flashing light that appeared, disappeared and seemed difficult to approach.

The available geographical evidence strongly supports the second interpretation. The sceptical case does not depend on observers having an unobstructed panorama across the Suffolk coast. Instead, it proposes that people moving through woodland near the forest's eastern edge would have experienced brief flashes through gaps between trunks and branches, with the light repeatedly vanishing as their position changed. Contemporary [witness statements]({{ 'witness-dates/' | relative_url }}), later site visits and the timing of the flashes all point in that direction, although critics argue that a distant lighthouse cannot explain every aspect of the wider Rendlesham story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## The line of sight from the forest edge

The critical geographical point is that the alleged landing area was not buried deep within dense woodland. Much of the activity occurred close to the eastern side of Rendlesham Forest, facing open farmland towards the Suffolk coast. Orfordness Lighthouse stood roughly five miles (about eight kilometres) to the east, on approximately the same bearing described in several witness accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

Investigations by science writer Ian Ridpath and local forester Vince Thurkettle found that from suitable positions near the forest edge the lighthouse could indeed be seen, not as a continuously visible beacon but as a periodic flash appearing between trees. Ridpath's night-time site visit was undertaken specifically to test whether the geometry made the explanation physically possible rather than merely plausible on paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

Terrain also mattered. The land between the forest and the coast is gently undulating rather than mountainous. This means the lighthouse itself was hidden below the horizon, while the rotating beam could still sweep into view at roughly eye level. Several investigators have noted that this arrangement makes a distant coastal light appear more mysterious than a lighthouse plainly visible on a shoreline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

## How trunks and branches create intermittent flashes

The lighthouse explanation does not require observers to see the beam continuously. In fact, intermittent [visibility]({{ 'visibility/' | relative_url }}) is one of its strongest features.

A rotating lighthouse beam already produces a regular cycle of illumination. When viewed through woodland, that cycle is further broken up by the geometry of the trees:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Individual trunks block most of the beam.
* Narrow gaps briefly reveal the light.
* Slight movements by the observer alter which gaps align with the lighthouse.
* Branches and undergrowth further interrupt visibility.

</div>

As a result, a fixed lighthouse can appear to blink irregularly or to "peek" through the forest. An observer taking only a few steps may lose sight of it entirely before seeing it again from another position. This behaviour matches descriptions of a light that repeatedly vanished behind the trees and then reappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

Importantly, the sceptical explanation does not claim that the forest became transparent. Rather, it argues that woodland naturally creates a sequence of fleeting sightlines. Even relatively sparse pine stands can produce this effect over long distances because only a tiny opening is needed for a bright lighthouse beam to become visible.

## Why movement can make a fixed light seem elusive

Human movement changes the geometry of sightlines surprisingly quickly at night.

When observers believe they are approaching a nearby object, every disappearance of the light can be interpreted as movement by the object rather than by the observer. In woodland this is especially easy because reference points are limited, distances are difficult to judge and darkness removes much of the visual information normally used to estimate range.

Several [first-night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}) witness statements fit this pattern. Witnesses reported following the flashing light only to discover that it remained much farther away than expected. Contemporary accounts include descriptions of reaching open ground and eventually recognising that the source was a beacon or lighthouse rather than something inside the forest. These early statements are generally regarded as more significant than later recollections because they were recorded much closer to the events themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

## The importance of the five-second flash

One of the strongest pieces of support for the lighthouse interpretation is the timing of the flashes.

During Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt's investigation on 28 December, his audio recording repeatedly refers to a flashing light low on the eastern horizon. Researchers later compared the recorded intervals with the operating characteristics of Orfordness Lighthouse and found a close correspondence: the lighthouse produced a flash approximately every five seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

Timing alone does not prove identity, but it is difficult to dismiss because it combines with the geographical alignment:

Supporters of the UFO interpretation often respond that experienced USAF security personnel would already have known where the lighthouse was and would not have mistaken it for an unidentified object. ## What the forest visibility evidence shows

The question is therefore not whether Orfordness Lighthouse was visible everywhere within Rendlesham Forest. The evidence indicates that it was not. Visibility depended on location, direction and the observer's movement through the trees.

What the available geographical and witness evidence does support is that observers near the eastern edge of the forest could obtain intermittent views of the lighthouse beam through natural gaps in the woodland. Those brief flashes, repeatedly appearing and disappearing as people walked through the trees, provide a practical mechanism for how a distant fixed beacon could initially seem like an elusive light within or beyond the forest, forming one important component of the broader cumulative conventional explanation of the Rendlesham Forest incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>
