## Introduction

One of the most frequently examined pieces of evidence from the Rendlesham Forest incident is Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s audio recording, made during the second night's investigation. Sceptics have argued that the recurring flashes heard and described on the tape correspond closely to the regular beam of the Orfordness lighthouse, while supporters of an [unexplained]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }}) interpretation argue that the observed light displayed additional behaviour that a lighthouse cannot account for. The timing comparison has therefore become a focused test of competing explanations: rather than asking whether every aspect of the incident can be explained by the lighthouse, it asks whether the specific flashing light recorded on the tape behaves as the lighthouse would be expected to behave. The result is significant because the tape is a contemporaneous record, reducing reliance on memories formed years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## What Halt Described on the Tape

As Halt's patrol moved through the forest, attention eventually centred on a bright flashing light visible through the trees. On the recording, members of the group repeatedly comment on its brightness, apparent movement and intermittent [visibility]({{ 'visibility/' | relative_url }}). Through a starlight scope, Halt described a bright flash with what he called a "dark centre", comparing it to a pupil or an eye.

The recording also captures uncertainty. The observers repeatedly attempt to determine whether the light is moving, approaching or receding. At night, especially when looking through trees at a distant point source, such judgements are notoriously difficult because the surrounding landscape provides few reliable depth cues. A light alternately hidden and revealed by trunks and branches can appear to shift position even when fixed.

Importantly, the tape documents reactions as events unfolded rather than retrospective storytelling. That makes it one of the strongest primary sources from the incident, but it also preserves the uncertainty of the moment rather than providing a definitive identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold File...</span></span></span>

## The Five-Second Flash Comparison

The most widely cited sceptical analysis compares the timing of the flashes heard on Halt's tape with the operating cycle of the Orfordness [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}), situated several miles east of Rendlesham Forest.

Researchers, particularly astronomer Ian Ridpath, timed the intervals between the recorded observations of the recurring flashes and found that they closely matched the lighthouse's approximately five-second flash cycle. Investigations of the site also found that the direction in which Halt's party was looking aligned broadly with the lighthouse's bearing beyond the forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

The comparison rests on three linked observations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Direction:** the flashing light lay broadly eastwards, matching the bearing of the lighthouse.
* **Rhythm:** the repeated flashes occur at intervals close to the lighthouse's five-second cycle.
* **Persistence:** unlike an object manoeuvring through the forest, the source repeatedly reappears in a predictable pattern consistent with a rotating beacon.

</div>

The timing argument is stronger than a simple claim that "there was a lighthouse nearby." It attempts to match an independently known physical characteristic—the beacon's operating interval—with a contemporaneous recording made during the investigation.

Several later summaries of the case, including sceptical reviews and reference works, have treated this five-second correspondence as one of the strongest conventional explanations for the flashing light described on the tape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theblackarchive.net/en/case/011" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackarchive.net">[theblackarchive.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackarchive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">검은 기록 보관소The Rendlesham Forest Incident — CASE FILE 011 &#124; The Black Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">검은 기록 보관소The Rendlesham Forest Incident — CASE FILE 011 &#124; The Black Archive</span></span></span>

## Visibility Disputes and the Limits of the Match

Although the timing comparison is persuasive to many investigators, it does not settle every aspect of the recording.

Supporters of an unexplained interpretation argue that Halt and his team described more than a simple flashing beacon. They point to comments suggesting apparent movement, changes in brightness and later observations of lights elsewhere in the sky. Charles Halt himself has consistently rejected the suggestion that the lighthouse explains what he believed he observed, later arguing that the lighthouse was not in the same position as the principal light. Critics of that claim note that his later recollections differ from the geometry implied by the original recording and subsequent site reconstructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/Halt_affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Col Halt&#x27;s affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Col Halt&#x27;s affidavit</span></span></span>

Another area of debate concerns visibility. Some have questioned whether the lighthouse would have been visible through dense woodland. Site visits conducted after forestry changes, along with photographs taken from comparable viewpoints, indicate that gaps in the tree line can allow [the beam]({{ 'the-beam/' | relative_url }}) to appear intermittently. That intermittent visibility is itself consistent with a rotating lighthouse beam briefly emerging between trunks before disappearing again, potentially creating the impression of an isolated flashing object within the forest rather than beyond it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

Even so, the lighthouse explanation has limits. Matching the flash interval does not automatically explain every statement on the tape or every claim made about the wider Rendlesham incident. The comparison addresses one recurring light source observed during Halt's investigation; it does not, by itself, resolve reports of other lights, interpretations of their movement or later witness recollections.

## Why the Timing Test Matters

Within the broader discussion of expectation, stress and night-time misperception, the lighthouse timing test illustrates how an ordinary stimulus can become difficult to recognise under unusual viewing conditions.

A rotating beacon viewed across several miles at night is not perceived under laboratory conditions. Observers are moving, looking through trees, communicating with one another and attempting to interpret an unexpected situation. Regular flashes can therefore appear more complex than they are, particularly when distance and background references are poorly defined.

Because the tape was recorded during the investigation itself, the repeated five-second correspondence provides an objective feature against which competing explanations can be evaluated. It neither proves that every aspect of the Rendlesham Forest incident was a misidentification nor confirms that an extraordinary object was present. Instead, it demonstrates that at least one prominent component of the recorded observations is consistent with the independently documented behaviour, direction and timing of the Orfordness lighthouse, making it one of the strongest conventional explanations for that specific part of Halt's recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>
