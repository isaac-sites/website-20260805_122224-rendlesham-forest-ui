## Introduction

The Halt tape is the strongest real-time record from the Rendlesham Forest UFO incident, but it is not a recording of an alien craft. It preserves Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }}) and several other United States Air Force personnel examining ground marks, checking radiation levels and reacting to unusual lights during the second forest investigation in the early hours of 28 December 1980. That makes it unusually valuable: instead of relying entirely on memories recorded years later, researchers can hear what the party noticed, measured and believed while events were unfolding. The UK National [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}) describes it as a live recording made during Halt’s expedition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/briefing-guide-12-07-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesBriefing documentDuring the expedition Halt saw several unidentified lights and made a live tape recording of the incide...</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_halt_tape_evidence_a646b7-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Halt Tape" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

Its evidential value is nevertheless limited. The microphone captured voices, not the lights themselves; the recorder was repeatedly stopped; the radiation figures were low and difficult to interpret; and the most dramatic visual descriptions lack independent photographic, [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}) or instrument confirmation. The tape proves that a serious and increasingly puzzled military party conducted an investigation. It does not, by itself, establish what produced the phenomena they reported.

## How the Recording Was Made

Halt entered the forest after hearing that lights had reappeared near the area associated with the earlier encounter. He carried a hand-held microcassette recorder of the kind he used for spoken notes. Rather than producing a continuous surveillance record, he switched it on to document selected observations, measurements and changes in the party’s situation. The surviving audio lasts only about 18 minutes, although its spoken time references show that the investigation covered a much longer period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. The tape is often described as a recording “of the encounter”, which can suggest an uninterrupted soundtrack. In reality, it is closer to a field notebook dictated in bursts. Halt announces times, asks colleagues for readings, describes positions and records what he thinks he can see. Halt’s later memorandum condensed the incident into a formal summary, while subsequent interviews added interpretation and recollection. The audio preserves an earlier layer: what the team was saying before the episode had acquired decades of publicity and mythology.

## Key Moments on the Tape

The recording falls into three broad phases: examination of the alleged landing area, pursuit of a flashing light beyond the trees, and observation of star-like objects in the sky. Each phase contributes a different kind of evidence, and each has different weaknesses.

### The ground survey

Early on the tape, the party examines three depressions associated with the claimed landing site. Halt records their approximate arrangement and comments on abrasions or damage to nearby trees. A serviceman operating an AN/PDR-27 survey meter calls out [radiation readings]({{ 'radiation/' | relative_url }}) while moving across the site. The audio is useful because it demonstrates that measurements were genuinely being taken and that the men attempted comparisons between the depressions, the centre of the formation, nearby trees and surrounding ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

The figures, however, do not form a clear pattern of strong contamination. Readings commonly transcribed from the tape lie around 0.03 or 0.04 milliroentgens per hour, with a higher figure near 0.07 in one part of the site. The party also records brief increases away from the central depressions. Rather than revealing a sharply defined radioactive footprint, the audio captures small fluctuations close to the lower end of what the instrument could meaningfully resolve.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

This is an important example of the tape cutting in two directions. Supporters can point to a real-time record of higher readings at selected points. Critics can answer that the differences were slight, inconsistent and measured with equipment designed principally for radiation levels much greater than the subtle variations being sought. The tape confirms the readings were discussed; it cannot establish that they were scientifically significant.

The recording is similarly inconclusive about the ground marks and damaged trees. Halt’s remarks show that the features were present and attracted attention. Audio cannot determine when the depressions were formed, whether branch damage was recent, or whether either feature had any relationship to an aerial object. Without controlled sampling, precise mapping or an uncontaminated comparison site, spoken observations remain suggestive rather than diagnostic.

### The flashing red light

The most vivid portion begins when the party notices a red light beyond the forest. Halt describes it as resembling a red sun with a darker centre. Voices on the recording refer to movement, pulsing and apparent fragments or glowing material coming from it. At one point the group reacts as though the light has changed form or split into separate points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

These exchanges are central to the tape’s reputation. Halt does not sound as though he is calmly inventing a prepared story; he sounds engaged, uncertain and intent on following something he cannot immediately identify. Other voices participate, meaning the recording is not simply a solitary retrospective narration.

Yet the tape provides almost no reliable scale or distance information. A light viewed through trees at night can appear to move when observers move, when branches interrupt the line of sight or when atmospheric conditions change its brightness. The men repeatedly advance towards the phenomenon, but the audio does not demonstrate that they close the distance. This is compatible with a remote fixed source that initially seemed to be nearby.

A leading conventional explanation identifies at least part of this phase with the Orfordness [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}), situated beyond the forest in the same general direction. Analysis of intervals audible on the tape has found a roughly five-second flashing cycle, matching the lighthouse’s characteristic rate at the time. The earlier witnesses had also referred to a distant beacon or lighthouse-like light in that direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape-analysis2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

That correlation is substantial but not absolute proof. Halt insisted that what he observed behaved unlike a normal lighthouse, and the transcript contains descriptions more complex than a simple periodic flash. The evidential question is whether those additional impressions require an additional object, or whether movement through wooded terrain, variable visibility and heightened expectations transformed a distant beacon into something apparently mobile and structured. Because the tape contains no image or compass-controlled sightline, it cannot settle that dispute.

### Star-like objects and the reported beam

Later, the group reaches more open ground and observes bright, star-like objects in the sky. Halt records two towards the north and one towards the south. He describes them as coloured, elliptical through an optical device and apparently capable of moving or sending down light. Near the end comes the tape’s best-known line: the party is observing what appears to be a beam descending towards the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape3.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

The phrasing deserves careful attention. Halt says “what appears to be”, which records an interpretation rather than a measured fact. The microphone captures no physical effect associated with the beam, such as an impact, heat, electrical disturbance or reaction from a detector. Nor does the tape identify a point where the beam supposedly met the ground. The moment is compelling testimony, but it remains testimony.

Astronomical analysis has proposed that the southern object was Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, while other [bright stars]({{ 'bright-stars/' | relative_url }}) may account for the northern lights. Stars close to the horizon can scintillate strongly, flash in several colours and look distorted through binoculars or other optics, especially in unstable air. Small involuntary movements of a hand-held instrument can also make a stationary point seem to dart or describe shapes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape-analysis2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

The tape does not eliminate that explanation. Its wording repeatedly compares the objects with stars, and the reported southern light remains in roughly the same area for an extended period. Conversely, Halt and his companions regarded the changes as too pronounced to be ordinary stellar twinkling. The recording preserves that judgement but supplies no angular measurements, photographs or simultaneous astronomical observations with which to test it.

## What the Voices Establish

The most defensible conclusions from the tape concern the witnesses and their investigation rather than the physical identity of the lights.

First, Halt’s party was not merely reconstructing the night from memory. The men were moving through the forest, exchanging observations, reading an instrument and responding to changing sights while the recorder was operating. The audio therefore makes it difficult to dismiss the entire second investigation as a story invented many years later.

Second, the tape shows uncertainty rather than instant certainty. The speakers compare impressions, ask where lights have gone, discuss possible movement and struggle to describe what they see. That uncertainty is compatible with honest witnesses confronting ambiguous stimuli. It is not, however, evidence that the stimuli were technologically extraordinary.

Third, the recording creates a benchmark against which later claims can be checked. Accounts published decades afterwards sometimes contain sharper, more dramatic details than either the tape or Halt’s January 1981 memorandum. The contemporaneous record mentions a beam apparently coming down to the ground, for example, but does not document some later claims that a narrow beam landed immediately at the men’s feet or entered a sensitive weapons-storage area. Differences of this kind do not automatically invalidate every later recollection, but they give the earlier audio greater evidential weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span></span></span>

Finally, the recording demonstrates that rank is not the same as observational immunity. Halt was an experienced deputy base commander, and the presence of trained military personnel makes the case historically significant. Training can strengthen procedural reliability—such as recording times or taking meter readings—but it does not provide automatic protection against distance errors, astronomical misidentification or the visual effects of darkness and expectation.

## Why the Radiation Readings Remain Weak Evidence

The radiation sequence is sometimes presented as the tape’s objective core because it involves an instrument rather than eyesight. In practice, an instrument reading becomes strong evidence only when its operating range, calibration, survey method and background variation are properly understood.

The AN/PDR-27 was designed as a military radiological survey meter, not as a sensitive environmental laboratory instrument for mapping tiny changes in natural background radiation. At the settings discussed on the tape, the needle was close to the bottom of the scale. Small apparent differences could arise from normal fluctuations, imprecise needle reading, probe position or the instrument’s limited resolution. Technical critics have therefore argued that even the reported maximum was not extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hjkc.de/_blog/2598-ufo-forschung---ufo-absturz-bei-rendlesham-forest---teil-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hjkc.de">[hjkc.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hjkc.de</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Raumfahrt+Astronomie-Blog von CENAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Teil-1An AN/PDR-27 meter of the type used by Halt et al, shown switched to its lowest range, which Halt referred to on his tape recording...</span></span></span>

The field procedure also lacked controls that would be expected in a formal scientific survey. The tape records spot readings rather than a systematic grid. It does not establish how recently the meter had been calibrated, whether repeated measurements produced stable values, or whether equivalent soil and trees elsewhere in the forest gave the same range. A brief rise was also noted at a point well away from the supposed landing marks, weakening the argument that the fluctuations defined a unique triangular site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeThis is a transcript of the cassette tape recording made by Lt Col Charles Halt on the ni...</span></span></span>

This does not make the readings imaginary. The party clearly saw the meter respond and tried to document the figures. What it means is that the tape preserves raw observations without the experimental structure needed to turn them into persuasive physical evidence of an unknown craft.

## Limits of Audio as Physical Evidence

Audio is exceptionally good at preserving sequence, language, emotional tone and the interaction between witnesses. It is poor at preserving the properties most needed to identify a distant light.

The Halt tape does not directly record:

* the appearance, brightness, size or colour of the lights;
* their exact bearings, elevations or distances;
* the observers’ movements and changing lines of sight;
* the meter needle or the precise placement of its probe;
* the alleged tree damage and [ground depressions]({{ 'ground-marks/' | relative_url }});
* any beam striking the ground;
* an independent radar, photographic or optical record.

It also omits everything that occurred while the recorder was paused. The spoken time announcements show jumps between segments, so the surviving sequence cannot reveal whether observations were continuous, how long particular effects lasted without interruption or what alternative explanations were discussed off tape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/the-halt-tape-colonel-halts-audio-tape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Colonel Halt: Tell them negative at this time.... Accidentally Added over tape: He took this long to document…... Colonel Halt: 2.44: W...</span></span></span>

Nor is excitement a detector of objective strangeness. The urgency in Halt’s voice supports sincerity, but sincere observers can be mistaken—particularly at night, in unfamiliar terrain and after being told that colleagues had encountered a strange object in the same area. Expectation does not require hallucination; it can influence which distant light attracts attention, how movement is perceived and how ambiguous changes are described.

Conversely, the tape’s limitations should not be exaggerated into a claim that it has no value. It is far better evidence of the investigation’s reality than a polished recollection produced decades later. It preserves immediate descriptions that can be compared with geography, lighthouse timing, star positions and later testimony. Its weakness is not that it is merely anecdotal, but that it is a rich record of human observation without an equally rich record of the objects being observed.

## What Can the Halt Tape Really Prove?

The tape can prove, to a reasonable historical standard, that Charles Halt led a serious night-time investigation; that several servicemen examined purported ground traces; that an AN/PDR-27 meter produced small variable readings; and that the group observed lights they found difficult to identify. It also shows that the account was not wholly reconstructed after publicity began. The National Archives’ own description of the material treats the recording as a live document of the expedition, alongside Halt’s subsequent official memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/briefing-guide-12-07-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesBriefing documentDuring the expedition Halt saw several unidentified lights and made a live tape recording of the incide...</span></span></span>

It cannot prove that the ground marks were made by a landed vehicle, that the radiation readings were abnormal, that the red light was physically moving nearby, or that the star-like objects projected real beams. Every one of those stronger propositions requires information the microphone did not capture.

The fairest assessment is therefore neither that the Halt tape is definitive UFO proof nor that it is worthless noise. It is unusually strong evidence that an extraordinary experience occurred at the level of perception, interpretation and military response. As physical evidence of an unknown aerial vehicle, it remains incomplete and compatible with a combination of low-level instrument fluctuations, a distant lighthouse, bright stars and the perceptual difficulties of moving through a dark forest. Its enduring importance lies in allowing listeners to hear the gap between those two propositions as it opened in real time.
