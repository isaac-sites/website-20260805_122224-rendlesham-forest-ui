## Introduction

One of the most frequently cited physical traces from the Rendlesham Forest incident is a series of circular abrasions on nearby pine trunks. A central question is whether those marks were fresh enough to have been created during the reported events or whether they pre-dated them. The surviving evidence does not resolve that question. In fact, the [contemporary record]({{ 'contemporary-record/' | relative_url }}) repeatedly shows uncertainty rather than confidence. Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }}) himself questioned whether at least one of the marks was old, and neither the field investigation nor later official documentation established when the bark damage had occurred. As a result, the tree abrasions remain an ambiguous observation rather than a securely dated piece of physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

## How the abrasions were described

The best contemporary description comes from the audio recording made by Charles Halt during the follow-up investigation in Rendlesham Forest. As the team examined the clearing, Lieutenant Bruce Englund remarked that several trees facing the centre of the alleged landing site appeared to have abrasions oriented towards the clearing. The suggestion was that the marks shared a common direction, making them potentially significant if they had all been created at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

However, the recording immediately introduces uncertainty. While inspecting one of the circular marks, Halt remarked that it "may be old" before commenting on the presence of what appeared to be fresh pine sap. Later on the tape he described another circular abrasion roughly three and a half to four inches across but again cautioned that it might not be recent. Rather than treating the marks as obviously new, the investigators themselves recognised that their age was uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

The distinction is important because later retellings sometimes describe the bark damage simply as "fresh burn marks" or imply that the marks clearly resulted from an unidentified craft. The original recording is more cautious. It documents observation and speculation rather than a confirmed forensic conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

## What clues might have dated the bark damage?

In principle, experienced forestry investigators can often estimate whether bark injuries are recent or old by examining several features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* whether resin is actively flowing or has hardened;
* the colour and moisture of exposed wood;
* weathering around the wound edges;
* evidence of insect activity or fungal growth;
* whether callus tissue has begun to form around the injury.

</div>

None of these indicators was systematically documented at Rendlesham. Although Halt referred to visible sap and instructed that part of the affected bark be sampled, no publicly available laboratory report establishes whether the resin or bark was analysed for age, unusual chemistry or heat damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

Likewise, the investigation produced no measured photographs with scales, no written assessment by a forestry specialist and no comparison with similar marks elsewhere in the plantation. Without those forms of documentation, later readers cannot independently assess whether the wounds appeared minutes old, days old or months old.

## Why pine sap was not enough to prove freshness

Some writers have suggested that visible sap demonstrates the abrasions must have been newly made during the incident. That conclusion goes beyond what the evidence supports.

Pine trees commonly produce resin after mechanical injury, but resin alone does not establish precisely when damage occurred. Depending on weather conditions, species and wound size, resin can remain visible for extended periods. A photograph or verbal observation that sap was present therefore cannot reliably date the original injury without additional examination.

The Halt recording reflects this uncertainty. Although Halt commented that he had rarely seen a pine react so quickly, he simultaneously questioned whether at least one abrasion might already have been old. His own remarks therefore combine an impression about the resin with explicit caution about the age of the underlying bark damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

## The competing explanation: forestry management marks

Sceptical investigators have long argued that the abrasions were not mysterious at all but ordinary forestry marks.

Forester Vince Thurkettle, who worked in Rendlesham Forest at the time, stated that the apparent bark damage consisted of axe marks made shortly before planned tree felling. Forestry staff commonly blaze selected trees by cutting patches of bark, exposing resin that can remain conspicuous afterwards. This interpretation was also discussed by Ian Ridpath during his reconstruction of the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

An important piece of supporting context is that many trees in the area had already been designated for harvesting before the UFO reports. A later Parliamentary answer confirmed that the Forestry Commission had selected and marked trees for felling well before the alleged incident and that their later removal formed part of normal forestry operations rather than an attempt to destroy evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written-answers/2001/apr/30/rendlesham-forest-tree-felling" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: api.parliament.uk">[UK Parliament API]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">api.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rendlesham forest tree felling</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UK Parliament APIRendlesham Forest: Tree Felling (Hansard, 30 April 2001)April 30, 2001...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2001</span></span></span>

This explanation does not conclusively identify every abrasion recorded on the Halt tape. Nevertheless, it demonstrates that an ordinary source of circular bark wounds already existed within the forest and therefore cannot be excluded.

## Why no firm age was ever established

The principal weakness of the trunk-abrasion evidence is not that one explanation has been proven wrong. It is that the marks were never investigated in a way that could reliably determine their age.

Several factors limit what can now be concluded:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **No baseline photographs** exist showing the same trees before the incident.
* **No dated forensic examination** of the bark was carried out.
* **No published laboratory analysis** established whether sampled bark or resin had unusual characteristics.
* **No detailed mapping** linked individual abrasions to the reported ground impressions.
* **The official memorandum** summarising the investigation did not discuss the age of the tree wounds or present them as established evidence of a recent impact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

</div>

These omissions mean that neither advocates nor sceptics can demonstrate the age of the abrasions from the surviving documentation alone.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_tree_damage_claims_3c6fb8_tree_marks_age_756112-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Trunk Marks illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_tree_damage_claims_3c6fb8_tree_marks_age_756112-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_tree_damage_claims_3c6fb8_tree_marks_age_756112-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 3</span></figcaption></figure>

## What the uncertainty means for the Rendlesham case

The unresolved age of the trunk abrasions limits their evidential value. If the marks had been conclusively shown to be fresh, consistently oriented and unrelated to forestry activity, they would have strengthened claims that an unusual physical event had occurred in the clearing. Conversely, if they had been demonstrated to be old forestry blazes, they would have ceased to support the [landing narrative]({{ 'landing-narrative/' | relative_url }}) altogether.

Instead, the evidence occupies an intermediate position. The investigators unquestionably observed bark abrasions, but the contemporary record never established when those wounds were made. Charles Halt's own repeated qualification that individual marks "may be old" is therefore one of the most important aspects of the surviving evidence. It shows that uncertainty about the tree damage was recognised at the scene itself rather than introduced only by later sceptical analysis.
