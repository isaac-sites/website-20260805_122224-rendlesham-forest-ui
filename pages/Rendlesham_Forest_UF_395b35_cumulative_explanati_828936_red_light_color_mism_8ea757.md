## Introduction

The fact that Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }}) repeatedly described the low eastern light as **red** is one of the strongest objections raised against the Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}) explanation. The lighthouse's principal beam was white, so at first glance the colour appears incompatible with the conventional account. However, colour alone does not decisively rule out the lighthouse. The question is whether witnesses at several miles' distance, observing an intermittent light through trees at night, could reliably identify its true colour, or whether other red lights in the same direction may have influenced what they perceived.

The cumulative conventional explanation does not argue that the lighthouse itself emitted a red beam toward the observers. Instead, it suggests that colour judgements under the specific viewing conditions were unreliable and that the eastern horizon contained more than one light source. The colour objection therefore weakens the lighthouse hypothesis, but it does not by itself falsify it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## What witnesses called red

The [contemporary record]({{ 'contemporary-record/' | relative_url }}) contains repeated references to a reddish light. During the second investigation, Halt's audio recording describes a flashing light low to the east, at one point comparing it to a "red eye". His later memorandum similarly refers to a "red sun-like light". These descriptions have become central to arguments that the object could not have been the lighthouse because the Orfordness beacon produced a white flash.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

Yet the descriptions are not entirely uniform. The witnesses were also trying to describe apparent movement, brightness changes and intermittent visibility rather than providing a precise colour measurement. On the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}), some of the earliest [witness statements]({{ 'witness-dates/' | relative_url }}) characterised the distant light simply as a flashing beacon or even explicitly as a lighthouse when they realised it remained far away. That variation suggests the colour description was only one aspect of a more complex visual impression rather than a consistently observed physical property.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## Why distant night colours are unreliable

A distant point light seen at night is a poor candidate for accurate colour judgement.

Several factors reduce colour reliability:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Low light levels.** Human vision shifts towards rod-dominated night vision, which is much less capable of distinguishing colours than daylight vision.
* **Atmospheric effects.** Moisture, haze and aerosols can scatter light and alter its apparent hue, especially close to the horizon.
* **Intermittent viewing.** The lighthouse was visible only during brief flashes, often through gaps in branches, giving observers only fractions of a second to judge colour.
* **Contrast effects.** Against a dark woodland background, changes in brightness can be interpreted as changes in colour, particularly when observers are already expecting an unusual object.

</div>

These effects do not mean that a white light necessarily appears red. Rather, they mean that colour estimates become significantly less dependable under precisely the conditions present in Rendlesham Forest. The sceptical argument therefore treats the reported redness as descriptive rather than definitive evidence against the lighthouse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

Another point is that witnesses were rarely observing an unobstructed lighthouse beam. Ridpath's site investigations showed that the flashes appeared and disappeared between trees and seemed to shift position as observers moved. Such fragmented glimpses are known to make distant lights appear less stable in both position and colour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## Alternative red lights in the same direction

The eastern skyline did not contain only the lighthouse.

Ridpath's photographs from the relevant viewing direction show additional lights visible beyond the forest, including prominent red warning lights on tall aerial structures at Orfordness. These aviation obstruction lights occupied broadly the same coastal area as the lighthouse, meaning observers looking east could potentially see several unrelated lights superimposed within a relatively narrow field of view.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

This possibility matters because witnesses were not making controlled observations with fixed bearings or optical instruments. A flashing white lighthouse beam, steady red obstruction lights and other distant coastal lighting could blend into a changing visual scene, particularly as observers walked through uneven woodland. The conventional explanation therefore allows for more than one ordinary light source contributing to the overall impression.

Importantly, this is not the same as claiming the lighthouse itself emitted a red flash. Rather, it proposes that the reported "red light" may have been influenced by nearby red aviation lights, colour adaptation, or alternating glimpses of different sources in the same general direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## How much weight should the colour objection carry?

The colour issue remains a legitimate criticism of a simplified "it was just the lighthouse" explanation. If the only observed light had been a consistently bright, unmistakably red object, the mismatch with a white lighthouse beam would require a stronger alternative explanation.

However, the broader conventional reconstruction does not depend on perfect colour agreement. It relies on a combination of factors:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the light lay in the same general direction as the Orfordness Lighthouse;
* it flashed with approximately the lighthouse's known interval;
* it appeared and disappeared behind trees in a manner consistent with intermittent line of sight;
* contemporary witness statements evolved from descriptions of a distant beacon towards more elaborate later recollections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

</div>

Within that cumulative framework, the reported red colour becomes a complication rather than a decisive refutation. It raises an important question about [perception]({{ 'perception/' | relative_url }}) but does not outweigh the geographical alignment, flashing rhythm and observational circumstances that continue to support the lighthouse as at least part of the conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>
