## Introduction

The strongest contemporary evidence for claims of broken branches above the alleged Rendlesham Forest landing site comes not from later witness recollections but from Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s audio recording made during his night-time investigation. On that tape, Halt briefly notes an opening in the pine canopy and "freshly broken pine branches" lying on the ground beneath it. This observation has often been cited as physical evidence that an object descended through the trees. However, the recording documents only a field impression, not a measured forensic finding. It contains no systematic survey of the branches, no mapping of their positions, no photographs demonstrating where they originated, and no examination capable of linking the damage to a descending object rather than ordinary woodland processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[Avalon Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. Several aspects of this statement deserve attention.

First, Halt clearly distinguishes between what he directly observed and what he estimated. He observed an opening in the canopy and broken branches on the forest floor. The estimate that they had broken off from approximately 15 to 20 feet above was an on-site visual judgement rather than a measured determination. The tape records no attempt to verify the height by climbing, measuring, or identifying matching break points in the trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[Avalon Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. Halt’s tape Transcribed bDecember 7, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2019</span></span></span>

Second, the branches are described only in broad terms. They were "small branches" roughly an inch or less in diameter. No species-specific description, moisture condition, bark damage, or fracture appearance is recorded. ## What Was Observed and What Was Inferred?

The Halt tape contains both observations and interpretations, and separating the two is essential.

The observations are straightforward:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an opening visible in the tree canopy;
* broken pine branches lying beneath that opening;
* Halt's impression that the branches appeared fresh;
* an estimate that some originated 15 to 20 feet above ground;
* an estimated branch diameter of about one inch or less.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[Avalon Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. Halt’s tape Transcribed bDecember 7, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2019</span></span></span>

</div>

The broader inference—that an unidentified object descended through the canopy—is not stated on the tape. Rather, it developed later as investigators and commentators attempted to connect the broken branches with the alleged landing site below.

The recording itself never documents a continuous damage path from the canopy to the ground. It does not identify impact scars on successive branches, snapped trunks, or a clear trajectory through the trees. Instead, the canopy opening and broken branches are noted as features present at the location. Whether they were related to the same event remained an open question.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[avalonlibrary.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. ## What Information Was Never Recorded?

The evidential limitation of the branch observation lies less in what was reported than in what was omitted.

The tape provides no record of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* how many broken branches were present;
* exactly where each branch lay;
* whether all branches originated from the same tree;
* whether matching fracture points could be identified overhead;
* the direction in which branches had broken;
* whether fractures appeared fresh when examined closely;
* whether photographs with scale documented the branch positions;

## Comparison with Halt's Official Memorandum

An important comparison comes from Halt's memorandum to the UK Ministry of Defence, written on 13 January 1981.

## Why the Branches Remain Ambiguous

Supporters of the Rendlesham incident often point to the broken branches as one component of a broader pattern that included ground impressions, tree abrasions and unusual lights. Critics note that the branches themselves were never documented in sufficient detail to establish an extraordinary cause.

The tape does not demonstrate that:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the branches broke during the reported event;
* they all resulted from a single incident;
* they were mechanically consistent with a descending object;
* they were unusual for that section of forest.

</div>

Likewise, sceptical explanations cannot conclusively identify the specific cause of every broken branch because the necessary physical evidence was never preserved. Instead, the available record simply remains too incomplete to support either a definitive extraordinary explanation or a definitive mundane reconstruction based solely on the branches themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[avalonlibrary.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. ## The Evidential Value of the Halt Tape

The significance of the Halt tape is that it preserves a real-time observation made before decades of later retelling. That gives the reference to broken branches greater historical value than many subsequent recollections.

At the same time, the recording illustrates the limits of contemporaneous field observations. Halt documented that broken branches existed beneath a canopy opening and estimated their apparent origin, but he did not gather the measurements or physical documentation that would be needed to demonstrate a causal connection with the alleged landing site.

As a result, the "freshly broken branches" remain a genuine contemporary observation rather than verified physical proof of a craft descending through the trees. The tape establishes that investigators noticed damaged branches; it does not establish what broke them or when.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[avalonlibrary.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col.
