## Introduction

Reports from the Rendlesham Forest incident include descriptions of lights that appeared oval, segmented, multicoloured or otherwise structured rather than as simple points. While atmospheric [scintillation]({{ 'scintillation/' | relative_url }}) can make a bright star flicker and change colour, the use of binoculars, image intensifiers and other night-viewing equipment introduces an additional set of optical effects. A distant point source can become enlarged, distorted or apparently divided into multiple components even though no physical structure has been resolved. This distinction matters because seeing a light acquire an apparent shape through optical equipment is not, by itself, evidence that the object itself possesses that shape.

## Why bright points are hard to focus at night

A bright star is effectively a point source of light. Unlike the Moon or a nearby illuminated object, it is too distant for ordinary binoculars or military night optics to resolve into a disc. Instead, the observer sees the optical system's response to a point source rather than the star itself.

This makes accurate focus unusually important. Even a slight focusing error causes the point to expand into a blurred pattern whose appearance depends on the design of the instrument, the observer's eyesight and the prevailing atmospheric conditions. Astronomers deliberately defocus bright stars during a "[star test]({{ 'star-test/' | relative_url }})" because the resulting rings and patterns expose optical imperfections that are invisible when looking at ordinary terrestrial targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/advice/airy-disc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyatnightmagazine.com">[Sky at Night Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyatnightmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky at Night Magazine Airy disks explained &#124; BBC Sky at Night Magazine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky at Night Magazine Airy disks explained &#124; BBC Sky at Night Magazine</span></span></span>

Night viewing makes precise focus more difficult for several reasons:

* Human pupils become much larger in darkness, increasing sensitivity to optical imperfections in both the eye and the instrument.
* Many military binoculars and image intensifiers are optimised for low-light detection rather than resolving fine detail from bright point sources.
* Bright stars often saturate image intensifier tubes, creating a bloom that obscures the true point of light.
* Atmospheric turbulence continually changes the apparent sharpness of the image, making perfect focus difficult to maintain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/advice/what-is-astronomical-seeing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyatnightmagazine.com">[skyatnightmagazine.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyatnightmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky at Night Magazine Seeing in astronomy &#124; BBC Sky at Night Magazine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky at Night MagazineSeeing in astronomy &#124; BBC Sky at Night MagazineMay 13, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2025</span></span></span>

Under these conditions, a witness may honestly report observing an oval, flattened or irregular luminous object even though the optics have never resolved any actual physical structure.

## How glare and internal reflections create false structure

Optical systems do not simply magnify light. They also generate artefacts when viewing extremely bright sources against a dark background.

### Defocus enlarges a point into an apparent object

When a star is out of focus, it ceases to appear as a tiny point and instead expands into a visible pattern. Depending on the optical system, this pattern may resemble a disc, concentric rings, a flattened oval or an irregular luminous patch. Small changes in focus can dramatically alter its appearance over only a few seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/advice/airy-disc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyatnightmagazine.com">[Sky at Night Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyatnightmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky at Night Magazine Airy disks explained &#124; BBC Sky at Night Magazine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky at Night Magazine Airy disks explained &#124; BBC Sky at Night Magazine</span></span></span>

To an observer unfamiliar with optical behaviour, this changing image can appear to be the object itself changing shape.

### Chromatic aberration separates colours

Lenses do not focus every wavelength at exactly the same distance. This optical limitation, known as chromatic aberration, produces coloured fringes around bright objects. A bright star may therefore appear to contain distinct red, green or blue regions rather than a single white light.

The effect becomes more obvious if the instrument is slightly out of focus or if the star is positioned away from the centre of the field of view. Astronomers frequently recognise these colour fringes as characteristics of the optical system rather than of the celestial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astro.berkeley.edu/faq/general/stars" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astro.berkeley.edu">[Astro Berkeley]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astro.berkeley.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astro Berkeley Stars &#124; Astronomy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astro Berkeley Stars &#124; Astronomy</span></span></span>

### Internal reflections and scattering

Every optical system contains multiple glass surfaces. A small fraction of incoming light reflects internally between these surfaces, producing faint secondary images, diffuse halos or asymmetric patches around a bright source.

In astronomical imaging these reflections are sufficiently well understood that specialist techniques are used to model and remove them from observations. Although far less pronounced during visual observation than in long-exposure photography, the same physical mechanisms can produce misleading impressions of extra lights or surrounding luminous structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3320" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Removing Internal Reflections from Deep Imaging Datasets</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Removing Internal Reflections from Deep Imaging DatasetsSeptember 17, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 17, 2009</span></span></span>

### Image intensifier blooming

Night vision devices amplify very weak light. Bright stars, however, can overload portions of the intensifier, causing the apparent image to spread beyond its true size. The resulting glow may appear soft-edged, elongated or surrounded by irregular luminous extensions.

This blooming effect does not indicate that the distant source has expanded or possesses a structured surface; it reflects the response of the imaging system to an exceptionally bright point source.

## Why observers may perceive a mechanical shape

The combination of atmospheric scintillation and optical artefacts can produce surprisingly elaborate descriptions.

A bright star near the horizon already exhibits rapid fluctuations in brightness, colour and apparent position because its light is passing through a long, turbulent atmospheric path. When this unstable image is viewed through optics that introduce defocus, chromatic aberration or blooming, the enlarged image can appear to contain distinct coloured sections, protrusions or separate luminous components.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomy.com/observing/why-do-some-stars-appear-to-twinkle-while-others-dont/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomy.com">[astronomy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Magazine Why do some stars appear to twinkle while others don’t?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy MagazineWhy do some stars appear to twinkle while others don’t?...</span></span></span>

As the observer subtly changes viewing angle or focus, these apparent features also change. An elongated glow may rotate, coloured regions may swap positions, or the image may briefly appear split into two adjacent lights. Because the object itself remains unresolved, these changes arise within the optical system rather than at the distant source.

This is one reason experienced astronomers are cautious about interpreting apparent structure in bright stars viewed under poor seeing conditions. Optical appearance alone is not considered reliable evidence of an object's physical form.

## What optical descriptions do not establish

Descriptions such as "oval", "half-moon", "cluster of lights", "segmented", "mechanical" or "multicoloured" are often treated as evidence that observers saw a structured craft. Optical science shows that this inference is not justified solely by the reported appearance.

By themselves, such descriptions do **not** demonstrate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that the light had a solid physical outline;
* that individual coloured regions corresponded to real features;
* that apparent splitting represented multiple independent objects;
* that changes in shape reflected motion or transformation of the source.

</div>

Instead, they are entirely consistent with what can happen when a bright unresolved point is viewed through imperfectly focused optics under turbulent atmospheric conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://astro.berkeley.edu/faq/general/stars" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astro.berkeley.edu">[berkeley.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astro.berkeley.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astro Berkeley Stars &#124; Astronomy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astro Berkeley Stars &#124; Astronomy</span></span></span>

Within the wider discussion of the Rendlesham Forest incident, this mechanism addresses only one specific class of observations: distant bright lights examined under difficult nighttime viewing conditions, particularly through binoculars or night optics. It neither explains nor disproves every reported feature of the incident, but it demonstrates that an apparently structured appearance is not, on its own, evidence that a distant light was a structured object.

    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
