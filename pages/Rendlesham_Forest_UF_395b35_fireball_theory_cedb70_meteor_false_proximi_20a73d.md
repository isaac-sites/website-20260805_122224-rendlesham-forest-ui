## Introduction

One of the key mechanisms behind the [fireball]({{ 'fireball/' | relative_url }}) explanation for the first Rendlesham Forest alarm is a simple limitation of human vision: at night, people are remarkably poor at judging the distance of bright objects in the sky. A brilliant meteor hundreds of kilometres away can appear to descend just beyond a nearby tree line because the observer has almost no reliable depth cues. This does not require unusual psychology or faulty eyesight. It is a well-known consequence of viewing luminous atmospheric phenomena against a dark, featureless background, and it has repeatedly led witnesses—including experienced observers—to believe that a distant fireball landed nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/stargazing-and-observing/celestial-objects-to-watch/about-meteors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[Sky &amp; Telescope]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope About Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeAbout Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!November 1, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why the night sky hides distance

In daylight, the brain estimates distance using many visual cues: familiar object sizes, shadows, atmospheric haze and overlapping landmarks. At night, most of those cues disappear. A bright meteor is seen against a largely black sky, with few reference points to indicate whether it is tens of kilometres away or several hundred.

Fireballs are especially prone to this illusion because they are exceptionally bright. Although the luminous phase of most meteors occurs roughly 80–120 km above the Earth's surface, they may be visible from enormous distances. A fireball near the horizon can therefore be hundreds of kilometres from an observer while still appearing low over local trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/stargazing-and-observing/celestial-objects-to-watch/about-meteors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[skyandtelescope.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope About Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeAbout Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!November 1, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2012</span></span></span>

The problem becomes greater when the meteor is viewed close to the horizon. Because the line of sight travels through much more atmosphere, brightness and colour are altered, while the object appears visually compressed against the landscape. Without independent distance information, the brain naturally assigns the light to the nearest plausible location.

## How motion creates a false endpoint

The illusion is strengthened by the way a fireball disappears.

A meteor does not usually remain visible until it reaches the ground. Instead, the glowing plasma surrounding it fades while the object is still high in the atmosphere. If this fading occurs behind a distant ridge or simply beyond an observer's local tree line, the eye often interprets the disappearance as a landing.

Several factors combine to produce this false endpoint:

* **A downward trajectory.** Many fireballs appear to descend steeply towards the horizon, encouraging the impression that they are approaching the ground.
* **Loss of [visibility]({{ 'visibility/' | relative_url }}).** The meteor abruptly ceases glowing while still many kilometres above the surface.
* **Foreground masking.** Trees, hills or buildings block the final part of the observer's view, making the disappearance seem to occur immediately behind those nearby features.
* **Expectation of impact.** Once the brain interprets the path as descending, it naturally assumes the object continued beyond the visible obstruction.

Astronomy organisations frequently note that eyewitnesses report fireballs landing "just beyond the trees" or "over the next hill", even though later trajectory analysis places them far away. In reality, the luminous flight normally ends well above the ground, and any surviving meteorites enter an unlit "dark flight" before falling elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/stargazing-and-observing/celestial-objects-to-watch/about-meteors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[skyandtelescope.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope About Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeAbout Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!November 1, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why trained observers can still be misled

This visual error is not limited to inexperienced skywatchers. It arises because the human visual system is attempting to solve a problem with insufficient information.

Even pilots and other trained observers have occasionally judged brilliant meteors to be nearby hazards. Sky & Telescope notes documented cases in which airline crews altered course to avoid fireballs that were in fact around 160 km away. Training improves recognition of celestial objects, but it cannot create depth information that the eye simply does not receive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skyandtelescope.org/stargazing-and-observing/celestial-objects-to-watch/about-meteors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyandtelescope.org">[Sky &amp; Telescope]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyandtelescope.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sky &amp; Telescope About Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sky &amp; TelescopeAbout Meteors, Meteor Showers, Fireballs and More!November 1, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2012</span></span></span>

Military personnel investigating an apparent aircraft emergency would have faced additional pressures. If a brilliant light appeared to descend beyond the perimeter fence and vanish near the forest, the most reasonable operational assumption would have been that something had come down locally. The expectation of finding a crash site follows naturally from the apparent geometry of the observation, even if the actual event occurred far beyond the visible horizon.

1. A very bright meteor becomes visible over southern England.
2. From the observers' viewpoint, it appears to descend towards the forest horizon.
3. The light disappears as its luminous flight ends or passes beyond the visible tree line.
4. The disappearance is interpreted as a nearby descent rather than the end of a distant atmospheric event.
5. Security personnel investigate the apparent landing area but find no wreckage because nothing actually reached the ground there.

</div>

This mechanism does not prove that a meteor caused the initial sighting. Instead, it demonstrates that the perceived proximity of a descending light is not reliable evidence that the object was genuinely close. The absence of dependable night-time distance cues means a spectacular atmospheric event can convincingly imitate a local descent, providing a plausible explanation for why witnesses might sincerely believe they had seen something come down just beyond the trees.

    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
