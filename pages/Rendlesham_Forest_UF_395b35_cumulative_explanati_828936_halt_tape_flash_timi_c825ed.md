## Introduction

One of the strongest conventional arguments in the Rendlesham Forest case is not simply that the flashing light seen by Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }}) could have been the Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}), but that the timing of the reactions captured on Halt's own audio recording appears to follow the lighthouse's distinctive five-second flash cycle. The tape is important because it is a contemporaneous record made during the investigation rather than a later recollection. If the intervals between the observers' comments repeatedly coincide with the lighthouse's known flash period, that provides an objective point of comparison rather than relying solely on memory.

The timing evidence is not universally accepted as conclusive. Supporters of the UFO interpretation argue that the tape also contains descriptions of movement, beams and changing behaviour that they believe go beyond a distant lighthouse. The central question is therefore narrower: does the recorded rhythm of the observations match what would be expected from the Orfordness Lighthouse?

## What the tape records in real time

Charles Halt carried a cassette recorder during the expedition into Rendlesham Forest on the night of 28 December 1980. Unlike his later memorandum, the tape captures events as they unfolded, including pauses, bearings, changes in direction and spontaneous reactions from members of the patrol.

At several points the group reports a flashing light low towards the east. Halt describes it as being near the horizon, "off to the coast", and visible at approximately 110–120 degrees—roughly the direction of Orfordness from their position. These geographical references are significant because the lighthouse lay east of the forest and was one of the few prominent light sources in that direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The SkepticApril 2, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 2, 2006</span></span></span>

The recording also contains repeated gaps during which the observers wait for the light to reappear before commenting again. Those intervals are the basis of the flash-cycle comparison.

## Comparing reactions with five-second flashes

The Orfordness Lighthouse used a rotating beacon with a flash period of approximately [five seconds]({{ 'five-seconds/' | relative_url }}) at the time of the incident. Sceptical investigators, particularly astronomer Ian Ridpath, noted that many of the pauses between the patrol's reactions are close to this interval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The SkepticApril 2, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 2, 2006</span></span></span>

The comparison works as follows:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* A light flashes.
* Someone on the tape reacts immediately.
* Several seconds pass with no comment.
* Another reaction occurs as the light becomes visible again.

</div>

When this pattern repeats over multiple observations, it resembles what would be expected if the observers were watching a lighthouse beam sweeping across their line of sight rather than a continuously illuminated object.

Because the tape is continuous rather than reconstructed afterwards, investigators can estimate these intervals directly instead of relying on witness recollection. Several independent sceptical analyses have concluded that the cadence is broadly consistent with the lighthouse's known flash period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-the-truth-about-rendlesham/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[skeptic.org.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: The Truth about Rendlesham - The SkepticApril 2, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 2, 2006</span></span></span>

## Where the timing match is strongest

The timing argument is strongest during the portions of the recording in which the observers remain stationary while watching a distant flashing light near the horizon.

Several features reinforce one another:

* **Regular intervals.** The comments tend to recur at roughly five-second spacing rather than at random intervals.
* **Correct bearing.** Halt repeatedly places the light towards the east-south-east, the direction of Orfordness.
* **Horizon position.** The tape includes remarks indicating that the light is low and appears to be "off to the coast", consistent with a distant beacon rather than an overhead object.
* **"Eye winking" description.** Halt compares the light to "an eye winking at you", language that naturally describes a periodic flash rather than continuous illumination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianThe Rendlesham Forest mystery: ‘It’s the perfect storm of a UFO case’ &#124; UFOs &#124; The GuardianApril 30, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2026</span></span></span>

Taken together, these observations form one of the more objective elements of the cumulative conventional explanation because they rely on measurable timing and geography rather than subjective impressions alone.

## Where the timing match is weakest

The five-second correspondence is not a complete explanation for everything heard on the tape.

The recording also contains statements that witnesses interpreted as indicating motion, changes in brightness and, at later stages, [apparent beams]({{ 'apparent-beams/' | relative_url }}) of light or "star-like" objects in other parts of the sky. These descriptions are not themselves explained merely by matching the lighthouse's flash interval.

Critics of the lighthouse explanation also point out that the tape was not recorded under laboratory conditions. Excited speech does not occur with stopwatch precision, background conversation overlaps observations, and some reactions may lag slightly behind the appearance of the light. Consequently, the comparison demonstrates consistency rather than exact synchronisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[The Cold File]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold File...</span></span></span>

## How the flash timing fits into the cumulative conventional explanation

Within the broader conventional reconstruction of the Rendlesham incident, the flash-cycle evidence is not presented as a complete solution to the entire case. Instead, it addresses one specific dataset: the flashing light recorded during Halt's later expedition.

The cumulative explanation proposes that different observations had different ordinary causes. In that framework:

* the initial alarm on the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }}) may have been triggered by a bright meteor or [fireball]({{ 'fireball/' | relative_url }});
* the repeatedly observed flashing light towards the east is consistent with the Orfordness Lighthouse;
* later "star-like" objects are interpreted separately as bright celestial bodies affected by atmospheric distortion.

The apparent five-second rhythm on the tape is therefore important because it links one part of the recorded evidence to a known physical source with a distinctive and independently verifiable flashing pattern. While it does not resolve every claim associated with the Rendlesham Forest incident, it remains one of the strongest pieces of evidence supporting the lighthouse component of the cumulative conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[thecoldfile.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold File...</span></span></span>
