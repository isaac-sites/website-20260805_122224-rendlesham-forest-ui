## Introduction

Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s memorandum of 13 January 1981 is the single most important official document describing the [ground depressions]({{ 'ground-marks/' | relative_url }}) associated with the Rendlesham Forest incident. It is also a notably brief document. While later retellings often present the marks as clear evidence of a landed craft, the memorandum itself records only a few specific observations: three depressions, their approximate dimensions, their location relative to the reported sighting, and radiation measurements taken later. It does not describe engineered impressions, provide a surveyed layout, or conclude that a craft had landed. Those stronger interpretations emerged largely through subsequent witness recollections and popular accounts rather than from the memorandum's own wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

> "The next day, three depressions 1½" deep and 7" in diameter were found where the object had been sighted on the ground."

He then added that, during a return visit on the following night, beta and gamma radiation measurements were taken. According to the memorandum, peak readings occurred in the three depressions and near "the center of the triangle formed by the depressions". It also noted moderate readings on the side of a nearby tree facing the depressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

Several features of this wording are significant:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The dimensions are explicitly approximate: about seven inches in diameter and one and a half inches deep.
* The memorandum states only that the depressions were found where the object had reportedly been seen.
* The phrase "triangle formed by the depressions" describes their arrangement but gives no measurements of spacing, orientation or symmetry.
* The document reports observations rather than drawing a formal conclusion about what created the marks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

</div>

The memorandum therefore establishes that three shallow ground depressions were observed, but it leaves unanswered many questions that would normally be important in identifying genuine landing impressions.

## Details the document did not record

A common misconception is that Halt's memorandum provides a detailed technical description of a landing site. It does not.

The memorandum contains no scaled sketch, map or survey showing the exact positions of the depressions. Readers are not told how far apart they were, whether they formed an equilateral or irregular triangle, or how their arrangement related to the reported orientation of the object. Without those measurements, it is impossible to reconstruct the geometry from the memorandum alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

Equally absent are descriptions that would help identify a mechanical origin. The memorandum does not mention:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* sharply defined edges;
* compression rings or load-bearing signatures;
* displaced soil characteristic of heavy contact points;
* metallic residues;
* scorching or heat damage;
* broken vegetation beneath the marks;
* laboratory examination of soil samples.

</div>

The document also says nothing about the weight that would have been required to produce the depressions, whether the impressions appeared to have been made simultaneously, or whether they resembled known landing gear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

Although plaster casts were reportedly made during the investigation, the memorandum itself neither mentions the casts nor discusses any analysis of their shape. The document therefore cannot be read as presenting forensic confirmation of landing gear or other manufactured supports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## The radiation measurements in context

The memorandum links the depressions with radiation measurements, but it does so in a descriptive rather than interpretative way.

It records background beta/gamma readings of approximately 0.1 milliroentgens, with peak readings in the depressions and near the centre of the triangle, plus moderate readings on one side of a nearby tree. The memorandum does not explain whether these values exceeded expected natural variation, nor does it claim they demonstrated contamination or exposure from an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>

That distinction is important because later discussions often combine the ground marks and radiation measurements into a single narrative of physical evidence. The original memorandum simply reports where readings were taken and where higher values were observed. It leaves interpretation to the reader.

## How later claims expanded the original description

Over the decades, the concise wording of Halt's memorandum became the foundation for increasingly detailed narratives.

Later accounts frequently described the depressions as definitive landing-leg impressions or as part of a clearly documented triangular footprint. Some witness recollections also connected the marks directly with a close encounter involving a triangular craft. These later descriptions often include details that are absent from the contemporaneous memorandum, such as precise geometry, implied engineering regularity or certainty that the marks were produced by a landed object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

This illustrates an important distinction between primary documentation and retrospective interpretation. The memorandum records observations made within weeks of the incident and is therefore valuable as a contemporaneous source. However, its brevity means that it cannot support every later claim attached to it.

For historians and investigators, the memorandum functions best as a baseline document. It confirms that three shallow depressions were observed and measured approximately, that they were regarded as relevant enough to investigate, and that [radiation readings]({{ 'radiation/' | relative_url }}) were taken around them. It does not, by itself, document the surveyed geometry or physical characteristics that would ordinarily be needed to identify the marks confidently as landing impressions from a mechanical object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJanuary 13, 1981...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 13, 1981</span></span></span>
