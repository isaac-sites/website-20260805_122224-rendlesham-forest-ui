## Introduction

[Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})’s investigation in Rendlesham Forest is the most valuable real-time record in the entire incident, but it is not a recording of a clearly observed landed craft. In the early hours of 28 December 1980, the deputy base commander of RAF [Bentwaters]({{ 'bentwaters/' | relative_url }}) and RAF Woodbridge led servicemen into the forest, examined ground marks with a military radiation meter and dictated observations into a handheld recorder. The surviving tape follows the party from faint meter responses and damaged trees to a blinking red light beyond the forest and several star-like objects in the sky.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_halt_night_investiga_d5a062-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Halt s Night" width="1024" height="559" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

The recording matters because it preserves what Halt and his companions said while events were unfolding, before decades of publicity and reinterpretation. It confirms that experienced military personnel were genuinely puzzled by unusual-looking lights. The recording naturally divides into three phases: examination of the alleged landing area, pursuit of a flashing light beyond the trees, and observation of distant objects in the sky. ### Ground marks, trees and radiation readings

At the beginning of the tape, Halt’s team inspects three depressions associated with the earlier incident. The men discuss branches that appear damaged or abraded and use the radiac meter at the marks, between them and elsewhere nearby. Halt records mostly small responses: several clicks, momentary changes and readings described as minor or stronger than the surrounding level. At one point the tape refers to a reading of roughly 0.07 milliroentgens per hour, compared with lower values of about 0.03 or 0.04 in other places.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape-analysis2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

### The red light beyond the trees

The investigation changed character when the group noticed a red light in the distance. Halt describes it as appearing sun-like, moving slightly and blinking through the trees. At moments it seems to him to approach, recede or throw off glowing fragments. The voices on the tape convey genuine excitement, especially as the party tries to maintain sight of it through uneven terrain and vegetation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeA transcript of Col Halt&#x27;s tape recording made during his sighting of the Rendlesham Fore...</span></span></span>

This is one of the recording’s most disputed passages because the apparent behaviour may have been produced by viewing a distant fixed or regularly flashing source through intervening trees. A light intermittently blocked by trunks and branches can appear to move, split or vanish as an observer changes position. The group’s own movement also complicates judgements of distance: a source on or beyond the horizon may seem to retreat whenever observers walk towards it.

The principal conventional candidate is Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}), several miles farther east near the coast. The line of sight from the forest placed the lighthouse in approximately the same direction as the red flashing light. That correspondence is strong evidence, though not a mathematical proof, that the lighthouse contributed to what Halt saw. Halt later rejected the explanation, arguing that he knew the lighthouse and that the object behaved differently. His professional confidence is relevant witness evidence, but it does not remove the alignment, flash-rate match or visual effects created by trees, darkness and changing viewpoints. The tape contains no range measurement, compass bearing precise enough to exclude the lighthouse, photograph or independent optical record of the red light.

The recording is therefore most persuasive as evidence of [perception]({{ 'perception/' | relative_url }}) under difficult conditions. It captures people attempting to describe a distant stimulus whose apparent size and motion they could not reliably establish. Their surprise is authentic; the physical nature of the source remains less certain.

### Star-like objects and the reported beam

Later, the party turns its attention to lights in the sky. Halt describes several objects, including two towards the north and a brighter one towards the south. They appear star-like, display changing colours and sometimes seem elliptical rather than round. In the most famous section of the tape, Halt says that the southern object appears to send a beam down towards the ground. He later records another light apparently positioned over or near Woodbridge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO – the Halt tapeA transcript of Col Halt&#x27;s tape recording made during his sighting of the Rendlesham Fore...</span></span></span>

Bright stars viewed low above the horizon can scintillate dramatically. Atmospheric turbulence refracts different wavelengths by changing amounts, making a bright point appear to flash red, blue, green or white, change shape and move slightly. The effect is especially vivid when an observer watches through magnification, cloud, haze or moisture and has few stable visual reference points.

Astronomical reconstructions have identified Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky, as a plausible candidate for the prominent southern object. Other bright stars were visible in the northern part of the sky. This explanation fits the tape’s repeated use of “star-like”, the objects’ low apparent elevation and the absence of a clearly recorded departure. Halt’s January memorandum stated that the brightest object remained visible for two or three hours, behaviour more compatible with an astronomical source than a nearby manoeuvring vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

The apparent beam is harder to reconstruct because the tape records a visual judgement, not a measurable ray captured on film. A narrow-looking shaft could arise from haze, scattered light, optical flare or the impression formed when a bright scintillating source appears to extend downwards. The recording proves that Halt interpreted what he saw as a beam; it does not independently establish that a concentrated beam physically reached the ground.

## What the recording does—and does not—prove

The Halt tape is unusually strong evidence for a UFO case in one specific respect: it is a contemporaneous account by identifiable military personnel. It reduces the possibility that the entire second investigation was invented years later. The party went into the forest, used a meter, found modest variations, watched a flashing red light and observed coloured, star-like lights in the sky. Halt’s tone indicates that he found the experience genuinely baffling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ARendelsham.ogg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Rendelsham.ogg6 Aug 2025 — Charles Halt made the recording on a Dictaphone. Its greatest strength—immediacy—is also revealing. Halt’s live descriptions are less elaborate than many retellings. The red light is observed at a distance through the trees. ## How the memo reshaped the night

On 13 January 1981, Halt sent a one-page memorandum headed “Unexplained Lights” through official channels to the Royal Air Force liaison officer. It summarised both the first patrol’s encounter and his own later investigation. The memo mentioned three depressions, “minor readings” from a Geiger counter, a red sun-like object moving through the trees and three star-like objects in the sky. It reported that the southern object sent down streams of light and that the lights remained visible for several hours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

The memorandum gave the case institutional weight, but its form should not be misunderstood. ## How later claims compare with the tape

Over subsequent decades, Halt’s account became more definite. He came to describe fast-moving objects, sharply changing directions, laser-like beams reaching the ground and beams directed into or near the weapons-storage area. In 2010 he signed an affidavit stating his belief that the events were extraterrestrial and had been concealed by the United States and British authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape-analysis2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

Parts of this later version have roots in the original material. The tape does contain a statement that a beam appears to be coming down, and the memorandum mentions streams of light. It is therefore inaccurate to say that every beam claim was added decades afterwards.

The scale and precision of the later narrative are another matter. The surviving recording does not clearly document a beam striking immediately beside the group. Nor does it preserve radio traffic confirming beams entering a nuclear-weapons compound. Halt later said that reports about the storage area came through police radio communications, but those communications are not independently preserved on the cassette. The claim consequently rests on later recollection rather than the strongest contemporaneous evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/apr/30/the-rendlesham-forest-mystery-its-the-perfect-storm-of-a-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[theguardian.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the rendlesham forest mystery its the perfect storm of a ufo case</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the rendlesham forest mystery its the perfect storm of a ufo case</span></span></span>

Likewise, the tape’s aerial lights are described predominantly as star-like objects whose colour and shape appear to vary. Later descriptions of coordinated high-speed movements and sharp angular turns are substantially more specific. The January memorandum refers to rapid angular movements, but also says the objects were low on the horizon and visible for hours. That mixture illustrates the central interpretive problem: apparent movement by a scintillating point source can feel rapid and purposeful even while the source remains in the same general region of the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

The fairest way to assess these differences is not to choose automatically between “the later account is false” and “the recording omitted the most important events”. Memory can preserve the emotional certainty of an experience while changing its geometry, sequence and detail. Public discussion, repeated interviews and exposure to other witnesses’ stories can also consolidate separate observations into a more unified narrative. For evidential purposes, however, the tape and January 1981 memorandum deserve greater weight than claims first documented many years later.

## The lasting value of Halt’s night

Halt’s recorded investigation does not settle the Rendlesham Forest incident, but it narrows the responsible conclusions. It shows that the second night was not merely folklore: a senior officer and his team conducted a genuine field search and encountered lights they struggled to identify. It also shows why military status alone cannot convert observation into proof. The team lacked reliable distance estimates, specialised scientific instruments and independent sensor confirmation.

The physical evidence recorded that night is weak. The radiation readings were small, variable and methodologically uncontrolled. The tree damage and depressions were observed but never tied forensically to a machine. The red flashing light had a strong positional and timing correspondence with Orfordness Lighthouse, while the prolonged star-like lights are compatible with bright astronomical objects distorted near the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/halttape-analysis2.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ianridpath.com.</span></span></span>

What remains compelling is the human record: calm procedure giving way to uncertainty as ambiguous sights become difficult to judge in darkness. The tape is valuable not because it clearly captures an extraordinary craft, but because it preserves the process by which experienced witnesses tried—and sometimes failed—to distinguish observation from interpretation. That makes Halt’s night the richest evidence within the Rendlesham story, and also one of the clearest demonstrations of its limitations.
