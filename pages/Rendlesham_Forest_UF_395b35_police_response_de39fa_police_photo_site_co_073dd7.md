## Introduction

A well-known photograph from the Rendlesham Forest incident shows a British police officer standing in the clearing where United States Air Force personnel believed an unidentified object had landed. The image is genuine and historically significant because it documents that police attended the location during the official response. What it does **not** demonstrate is that Suffolk Constabulary accepted the site as the confirmed landing place of an unknown craft. Over time, the photograph has frequently been reproduced without that distinction, allowing a record of police attendance to become, in some retellings, apparent evidence of official endorsement. Contemporary [police records]({{ 'police-record/' | relative_url }}), later correspondence from the investigating officer, and Ministry of Defence (MoD) files all point in the opposite direction: officers inspected the site but remained unconvinced that the ground marks represented a landed vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

## When was the photograph taken?

The best-supported provenance places the photograph in daylight on 26 December 1980, after the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }})'s reports of unusual lights. It depicts a British police officer alongside USAF personnel examining the small clearing where three shallow ground impressions had been identified. Versions of the image held in MoD files and discussed by researchers identify it as one of a series taken by Senior Master Sergeant Ray Gulyas during the daytime inspection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/photos-from-landing-site-december-26th.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">photos from landing site december 26th</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest IncidentPhotos from Landing Site December 26th, 1980...</span></span></span>

The National [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }})' commentary on the released UFO files is particularly important because it describes exactly what the photograph shows: a British police officer inspecting the alleged "landing" site. It does **not** state that the photograph documents a confirmed landing. Instead, the image appears in the archive alongside correspondence emphasising that the police role was limited and that witness accounts had grown more elaborate over the years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

The timing also matters. The photograph was taken after the reported nocturnal observations, during a daylight examination of physical traces. It therefore records an inspection of a location already identified by military personnel rather than an officer witnessing a landing or discovering a craft independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

## Why police presence is not the same as verification

Police officers routinely attend scenes to assess reports without endorsing the explanations offered by witnesses. In the Rendlesham case, Suffolk Constabulary responded because reports of lights near a sensitive military installation could have indicated a crashed aircraft, trespass, or another public-safety matter.

The surviving police documentation is notable for its restrained conclusions. During the initial night search, officers reported finding no unidentified craft and identified the only visible light as the Orfordness [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}). When they later examined the ground impressions, they did not conclude that a vehicle had landed. Contemporary accounts and later summaries indicate that the marks were regarded as consistent with natural causes, with one officer suggesting they could have been made by an animal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

This distinction is easy to overlook when viewing the photograph in isolation. An officer standing beside marked ground simply demonstrates that the location was examined. It does not transform the alleged landing site into an officially authenticated one.

## What the investigating officer later said

One of the strongest pieces of evidence against the "official confirmation" interpretation comes from correspondence written years after the incident by a Suffolk police inspector involved in the case.

A letter preserved in the MoD UFO files and summarised by The National Archives describes the police involvement as "minimal". More significantly, the officer wrote that witness testimony had become "substantially embellished" over the years. He also reiterated that beams from the Orfordness lighthouse could produce unusual visual effects under certain weather conditions based on his own experience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

Those comments are difficult to reconcile with the claim that the police photograph represents official validation of a landed extraterrestrial craft. Instead, they show that an officer associated with the original investigation regarded later retellings as increasingly exaggerated rather than increasingly confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

## How the image acquired a stronger meaning in later retellings

The photograph is visually compelling. It contains several elements that naturally suggest official investigation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a uniformed British police officer;
* military personnel examining marked ground;
* a clearly defined woodland clearing;
* visible stakes indicating the locations of the reported impressions.

</div>

Viewed without accompanying documentation, these features can encourage readers to infer that the authorities accepted the clearing as a genuine landing site. Yet none of those visual elements records the officer's conclusions. Photographs document who was present and what was visible; they do not capture the reasoning recorded in reports.

The image has therefore often been used rhetorically rather than evidentially. In books, documentaries and online discussions, it is sometimes presented as if the mere presence of a police officer constitutes independent confirmation. However, the associated documentary record consistently shows that the police examined the site without endorsing the extraordinary interpretation attached to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

## Reading the photograph alongside the documentary record

The most reliable way to interpret the image is to pair it with the surviving written evidence rather than treat it as a standalone proof.

Taken together, the records establish several points:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The photograph confirms that Suffolk Constabulary attended the reported site.
* It confirms that officers inspected the ground impressions in daylight.
* It does **not** show a landed object, wreckage or physical artefact.
* No police report concludes that an unidentified craft landed in the clearing.
* Later comments from the investigating officer describe the police role as limited and suggest that subsequent witness accounts became increasingly embellished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/podcast-transcript.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Archives UFO Files (August 2011)December 6, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2025</span></span></span>

</div>

The image therefore has genuine historical value, but its evidential weight is narrower than many later presentations imply. It documents official attendance at a reported location, not official acceptance of the extraordinary claim attached to that location.
