## Introduction

One of the most discussed discrepancies in the Rendlesham Forest incident is that Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s contemporaneous audio recording ("the Halt tape") and his official memorandum appear to describe different radiation results from the same forest survey. The tape captures a hesitant, uneven search in which low-level readings fluctuate and investigators comment on only minor increases. The memorandum, written about two weeks later, presents a far more orderly summary, describing distinct "peak readings" in the three alleged landing depressions and around their centre, with moderate readings on a nearby tree. Rather than indicating two different surveys, the contrast illustrates how a later narrative can simplify a complex sequence of field observations into a cleaner account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## What the memorandum claims

Halt's memorandum, dated 13 January 1981 and sent to the UK Ministry of Defence under the title *[Unexplained Lights]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }})*, states that investigators found:

* beta/gamma readings of **0.1 milliroentgens** in the area of the three [ground depressions]({{ 'ground-marks/' | relative_url }}),
* "peak readings" within the depressions and near the centre of the triangular arrangement,
* readings of approximately **0.05–0.07** on the side of a nearby tree facing the depressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Halt Memorandum.jpg - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

Presented in this form, the measurements appear to describe a coherent pattern centred on the alleged landing site. The wording naturally encourages readers to picture three related anomalies surrounding a single event.

Importantly, the memorandum does not describe the sequence in which readings were taken, how long [the meter]({{ 'the-meter/' | relative_url }}) was allowed to stabilise, or the scattered low readings recorded elsewhere during the survey. It functions as an executive summary rather than a field log.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## What the recorded survey actually captures

The audio recording paints a more complicated picture. As the team moves through the forest, Halt asks repeatedly for meter readings while another member reports what the instrument is doing in real time.

Instead of immediately finding a well-defined radiation pattern, the tape records comments such as "minor clicks" and uncertainty about whether particular depressions show anything unusual. One location attracts only minimal interest, another appears essentially inactive, and the strongest reported value is only a brief increase to roughly **0.07 mR/h**. Background readings discussed during the search are commonly reconstructed at around **0.03–0.04 mR/h**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[Avalon Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. Halt’s tape Transcribed bDecember 7, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2019</span></span></span>

The recording also shows that the investigators were exploring rather than documenting a predetermined result. They moved between the depressions, checked surrounding trees, and continued searching while discussing other phenomena in the forest. The conversation lacks the neat progression implied by the later memorandum.

Another notable feature is that the tape includes a comparable increase at a location well away from the supposed landing marks. If a similar fluctuation occurred elsewhere, it weakens the idea that every small rise in the meter necessarily marked something unique about the depressions themselves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

## Why the two accounts diverge

Several factors help explain why the tape and memorandum give different impressions without requiring deliberate falsification.

### A summary replaced a running commentary


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The tape is a contemporaneous record of investigators reacting to readings as they appeared. Such recordings naturally include uncertainty, repeated measurements, false starts and comments that later prove unimportant.

</div>

The memorandum instead compresses the investigation into a concise official report. In doing so, it highlights the readings that appeared most relevant while omitting much of the surrounding variability. This produces a cleaner pattern than listeners hear on the original recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

### Memory may have influenced the written report

Halt prepared the memorandum more than two weeks after the investigation rather than immediately afterwards. The document also contains known factual errors unrelated to the radiation survey, including dates that are shifted by one day. Researchers have argued that these mistakes show the memorandum was not written from a detailed contemporaneous notebook and therefore may also reflect imperfect recollection of the radiation measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

### Small fluctuations became emphasised

The AN/PDR-27 was a military survey meter intended primarily to detect significant radioactive contamination, not to distinguish tiny variations in natural background radiation. At the very bottom of its measuring range, brief changes in count rate could appear as noticeable needle movements even though they remained close to [background levels]({{ 'background-levels/' | relative_url }}).

Because the memorandum reports only the highest values, while the tape includes the lower readings and pauses between them, the written account naturally gives a stronger impression of a consistent anomaly than the real-time recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span></span></span>

## How later summaries can sharpen uncertain evidence

The difference between the tape and the memorandum illustrates a broader issue in historical investigations: contemporaneous records and retrospective summaries often serve different purposes.

The tape preserves uncertainty. Listeners hear investigators trying to determine whether individual readings matter, questioning one another, and moving between locations without immediately reaching a conclusion.

The memorandum presents conclusions rather than process. By selecting the apparent high points and arranging them into a concise narrative, it creates a pattern that is easier to understand but less representative of the uneven search documented on the recording itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://avalonlibrary.net/Rendlesham_Forest_incident_1980/Transcript%20of%20Colonel%20Halt%20Tape%20%28Ian%20Ridpath%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avalonlibrary.net">[Avalon Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avalonlibrary.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avalon Library Transcri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pt of Col. This distinction has shaped later debate. Supporters of the incident often cite the memorandum's reported peaks as evidence of physical traces, whereas sceptical analyses place greater weight on the contemporaneous tape because it captures the investigation before memory and later interpretation could reshape it.
