## Introduction

The Suffolk Constabulary records are among the most important pieces of contemporaneous evidence from the Rendlesham Forest incident because they were created during and immediately after the events, rather than years later from memory. They establish that police responded to reports from RAF [Bentwaters]({{ 'bentwaters/' | relative_url }}) and RAF Woodbridge, searched the reported area, checked for evidence of an aircraft emergency, and later examined alleged landing marks. They do **not**, however, establish that an unidentified craft landed in the forest, nor do they resolve every [military witness]({{ 'military-witnesses/' | relative_url }}) account. Their value lies in defining what an independent civilian authority actually observed—and, equally importantly, what it did not observe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

## Facts Directly Supported by the Police Records

The surviving Suffolk Constabulary paperwork documents a real official response to reports of unusual lights. At approximately 4.11 a.m. on 26 December 1980, police received a telephone report from RAF Bentwaters describing unusual lights beyond RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }})'s East Gate. The caller stated that unarmed military personnel had already been sent to investigate and that the incident was being described as a UFO in the literal sense of an unidentified flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The records also document several specific actions taken by the responding officers:

* They searched the reported area during the night.
* They contacted Air Traffic Control at West Drayton to determine whether a missing or distressed aircraft had been reported.
* Air Traffic Control had no knowledge of any aircraft corresponding to the report.
* The search produced no crashed aircraft, wreckage or [unexplained]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }}) object.
* Officers recorded that the only visible lights were those from the Orfordness [lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[vof.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

Later that morning, after military personnel reported a possible landing site, an officer returned to inspect the location. The police correspondence records three shallow ground marks but notes that they formed no clear pattern and appeared to have little depth. The attending officer considered that they could have been made by an animal rather than by a landed vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

These are straightforward factual observations recorded by officers carrying out ordinary policing duties rather than conducting a specialised UFO investigation.

## Claims the Records Do Not Corroborate

The police documents are often cited in debates about the incident, but they have clear evidential limits.

Most importantly, they do **not** confirm that a craft landed in Rendlesham Forest. Although officers examined the reported ground impressions, they did not interpret them as persuasive evidence of a landing. They recorded no damaged vegetation consistent with a heavy vehicle, no debris, no scorch patterns establishing an impact, and no recoverable physical material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

Nor do the records corroborate later descriptions of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a structured metallic object;
* close-range interaction with an unidentified craft;
* extraordinary surface markings;
* evidence of occupants;
* recovered artefacts or physical traces beyond the shallow impressions.

</div>

Those claims originate from military witnesses and later testimony rather than from the contemporaneous police paperwork.

Equally, the records do **not** disprove every witness account. The officers arrived after the initial military observations had begun and conducted a limited search aimed primarily at determining whether there had been an aircraft accident or public safety emergency. A negative search cannot establish that every earlier observation was mistaken or impossible. It establishes only that police themselves found no corroborating physical evidence during their attendance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why Contemporaneous Records Carry Particular Weight

Historians and investigators generally give substantial weight to records created during an event because they are less vulnerable to later memory changes, publicity and retrospective interpretation.

The Suffolk [police records]({{ 'police-record/' | relative_url }}) possess several characteristics that increase their evidential value:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* they were produced immediately after the reported events;
* they were created for routine administrative purposes rather than publication;
* they record operational actions as well as observations;
* they contain both positive findings and negative findings rather than attempting to construct a narrative.

</div>

This does not make them infallible. Police officers can overlook evidence, misidentify lights or conduct incomplete searches. However, their notes provide an independent civilian account that is separate from later military recollections and subsequent UFO literature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[Vetenskap och Folkbildning]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>

The distinction is especially important because many well-known details associated with the Rendlesham incident emerged through later interviews, books and documentaries. The police documents provide a fixed reference point against which those later claims can be compared.

## Later Commentary Remained Consistent with the Original Record

Subsequent correspondence from Suffolk Constabulary did not substantially alter the original assessment.

A 1983 police letter summarised the force's involvement as limited to the initial telephone report and two visits to the site. It reiterated that the night search produced negative results, that no aircraft was identified through Air Traffic Control, that the only lights observed were from Orfordness lighthouse, and that the shallow ground marks were thought capable of having been made by an animal. ## The Practical Evidential Limit

The Suffolk Constabulary records establish several important facts with confidence: police received a genuine report, responded promptly, searched the area, checked for a possible aircraft emergency, inspected the alleged landing site and found no convincing physical evidence that an unidentified craft had landed.

At the same time, the records cannot answer the broader question of exactly what the American servicemen experienced before police arrived. Their silence on a landing is not proof that nothing unusual occurred, but neither can it be treated as support for later claims that exceed what the officers actually documented.

For that reason, the police paperwork functions best as an evidential boundary. It confirms a real official response and carefully records negative findings, while leaving unresolved the separate question of how every military witness interpreted the unusual lights reported during the Rendlesham Forest incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vof.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/unusuallights.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vof.se">[vof.se]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vof.se</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vetenskap och Folkbildning000001March 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span>
