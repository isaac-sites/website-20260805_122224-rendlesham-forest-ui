## Introduction

Charles Halt's memorandum, titled **"[Unexplained Lights]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }})"** and dated **13 January 1981**, is the central official document describing the later phase of the Rendlesham Forest incident. It is one of the most important [contemporary records]({{ 'contemporary-record/' | relative_url }}) because it was written by the deputy base commander only a short time after the events and submitted through official channels. However, its evidential value is often overstated. The memorandum preserves a concise summary of what Halt and others reported seeing, but it is not a detailed observation log, an instrumented survey or a complete reconstruction of the night's events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

For the specific question of the later aerial lights, the memorandum establishes that trained military personnel reported unusual luminous objects and records several broad observational characteristics. What it does **not** establish is equally important. It lacks the precise measurements needed to determine whether the lights were astronomical objects, aircraft, atmospheric phenomena or something else with high confidence.

## When and why the memorandum was written

The memorandum was prepared roughly two weeks after the events and sent to the Ministry of Defence under the subject line **"Unexplained Lights."** It was intended as an official report rather than as a scientific paper or intelligence assessment. The document became part of the Ministry of Defence files that are now preserved by The National [Archives]({{ 'archives/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

That timing gives the memorandum significant evidential strengths:

## What the memorandum preserves about the aerial lights

For the later phase of the incident, the memorandum preserves several observations that are directly relevant to evaluating the reported lights.

It records that, after earlier activity near the forest, three **"star-like"** objects became visible. Two appeared to the north and one to the south, each roughly **10 degrees above the horizon**. The memorandum further states that the objects displayed red, green and blue colours, appeared to move rapidly with angular motions, and that the southern object seemed brightest. It also records the impression that the southern light intermittently directed a beam or stream of light downward.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/docs/810113_Halt_Memo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Halt Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Halt MemoNovember 10, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 10, 2025</span></span></span>

These details are valuable because they establish several points that later discussions cannot simply invent:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* observers distinguished multiple separate lights rather than describing a single object;
* the lights were described as being in different parts of the sky;
* colour changes formed part of the original written account;
* the lights remained visible long enough for extended observation rather than being instantaneous flashes.

</div>

Those features provide genuine historical evidence about what was reported, regardless of which explanation ultimately proves correct.

## Why the memorandum cannot resolve the astronomical question

The memorandum's greatest limitation is that it records impressions rather than measurements.

Although it mentions approximate direction and elevation, it does **not** include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* precise compass bearings;
* repeated angular measurements over time;
* exact times for individual observations;
* estimated angular velocities;
* distances or triangulation;
* photographs;
* telescope observations;
* calibrated optical measurements.

</div>

Without that information, later investigators cannot reconstruct the sky with enough precision to demonstrate that every reported light corresponded to a particular celestial object.

For example, the memorandum's statement that an object was "about 10 degrees above the horizon" provides only a rough estimate. Even an error of several degrees could substantially alter attempts to match a reported light with [Sirius]({{ 'sirius/' | relative_url }}) or another bright star, particularly when combined with uncertainty about the exact observation time.

Similarly, descriptions of movement are qualitative rather than quantitative. The memorandum reports rapid motion but does not specify whether the objects changed absolute position against the stars, appeared to drift because of observer movement, or merely seemed to move relative to the viewer. Those distinctions are crucial for astronomical reconstruction but cannot be recovered from the document itself.

## The importance of what the memorandum does not record

Readers sometimes assume the memorandum contains a complete account of the investigation. It does not.

The document is notably brief and omits many details that would be expected in a formal observational record, including:

* weather conditions throughout the observations;
* [visibility]({{ 'visibility/' | relative_url }}) estimates;

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* cloud cover during each reported event;
* precise observer locations;
* individual witness-by-witness accounts;
* sketches showing exact sight lines;
* instrument calibration information.

</div>

Because these data are absent, later investigators must combine the memorandum with other surviving evidence—including the contemporaneous audio recording, witness testimony and astronomical reconstruction—to evaluate competing explanations. The memorandum alone cannot perform that task.

## What the memorandum can establish with confidence

Used carefully, the memorandum supports several conclusions that are much firmer than debates over the identity of the lights.

It establishes that:

* Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }}) considered the events important enough to report formally.

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Multiple military personnel reported unusual lights during the investigation.
* Halt himself described later aerial objects as **"star-like."**
* The reported lights were low above the horizon and displayed changing colours.
* At least one light appeared sufficiently unusual to observers that Halt recorded the impression of a downward beam.

</div>

These are documentary facts about what was reported, not proof that the observations represented extraordinary physical objects.

## How historians and investigators should treat the memorandum

The memorandum occupies an unusual position in the Rendlesham evidence.

It is considerably stronger than memories recorded decades later because it is a near-contemporary official document produced within the military reporting system. At the same time, it is substantially weaker than an instrumented observation log because it lacks the quantitative information needed for rigorous reconstruction. The National Archives itself presents the memorandum as the principal official report associated with the incident, while noting that the Ministry of Defence's investigation concluded there was nothing indicating a matter of [defence significance]({{ 'defence-significance/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives...</span></span></span>

For the specific debate over the later aerial lights, the memorandum therefore occupies a middle ground. It is indispensable because it preserves the earliest official written description of the observations. Yet its compressed wording, approximate descriptions and absence of precise measurements prevent it from decisively confirming either an astronomical explanation or an unidentified physical craft. The document tells us reliably **what Halt reported**, but only partially **what was actually present in the sky**.
