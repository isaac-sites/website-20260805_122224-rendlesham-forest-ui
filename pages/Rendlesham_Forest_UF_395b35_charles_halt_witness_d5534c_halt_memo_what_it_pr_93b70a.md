## Introduction

Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s one-page memorandum, dated 13 January 1981 and headed **"[Unexplained Lights]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }})"**, is the single most important contemporary official document associated with the Rendlesham Forest incident. It is frequently cited as proof that the United States Air Force or the British government confirmed the landing of an extraterrestrial craft. That is not what the document establishes.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_charles_halt_witness_d5534c_halt_memo_what_it_pr_93b70a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Memo Meaning illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_charles_halt_witness_d5534c_halt_memo_what_it_pr_93b70a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_charles_halt_witness_d5534c_halt_memo_what_it_pr_93b70a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

What the memorandum does prove is more limited, but still historically significant. It demonstrates that a senior United States Air Force officer formally reported that security personnel and members of his own investigation had observed unusual lights and other unexplained phenomena near RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }}), and that he considered those reports important enough to forward through official channels. It records claims made by witnesses and observations made during the investigation; it is not an official finding that those claims were verified or explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

## Claims Recorded in the January Memorandum

The memorandum summarises two related episodes from late December 1980 rather than presenting the results of a completed investigation. Written in concise military language, it records what Halt believed had been reported and observed.

Among the principal claims included are:

* Security policemen reported seeing a glowing object in Rendlesham Forest that appeared triangular, metallic and illuminated.
* Three depressions in the ground were later found at the reported location.
* [Radiation readings]({{ 'radiation/' | relative_url }}) were taken around those depressions and on a nearby tree.
* During Halt's own night-time investigation, members of his party observed moving coloured lights beyond the forest.
* Later in the evening, several brighter objects were reportedly seen in the sky, with one appearing to project a beam of light towards the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

The document therefore provides direct evidence that these observations were formally recorded by a serving deputy base commander shortly after the events. It is not based on decades-later recollections, making it a particularly valuable historical source.

Just as importantly, the memo is written as a report of observations rather than as a conclusion. It repeatedly describes what witnesses "observed", "reported" or "appeared" to see instead of asserting that investigators had established the identity or origin of the phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## Why an Official Document Is Not an Official Finding

One of the most persistent misunderstandings is that because Halt's memorandum is an official military document, it represents an official military conclusion. Those are very different things.

An official report documents information received or observed during an incident. An official finding would require an investigation reaching a formal conclusion about what occurred. Halt's memorandum performs the first function but not the second.

The document itself is headed **"Unexplained Lights"**, signalling that the events had not been identified when the report was written. It contains no statement that an alien craft landed, no declaration that an unknown vehicle was confirmed, and no recommendation that extraordinary conclusions had been reached.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

The subsequent British Ministry of Defence handling reinforces this distinction. After reviewing the memorandum, officials concluded that it contained nothing suggesting a threat to the defence of the United Kingdom. Consequently, no formal investigation of the type often imagined by later writers was initiated. The Ministry's position was not that every reported light had been explained, but that the information presented no [defence significance]({{ 'defence-significance/' | relative_url }}) requiring further action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK Parliament...</span></span></span>

This administrative response is historically important because it demonstrates that the memorandum entered official records without becoming an official endorsement of its most extraordinary implications.

## Details Absent from the Contemporary Report

The evidential value of the memorandum is also defined by what it does **not** contain.

Many claims commonly associated with the Rendlesham Forest incident became prominent only through later witness interviews, books, documentaries and public lectures. Several of the most dramatic elements are absent from Halt's January 1981 memorandum.

The contemporary report does not include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a conclusion that an extraterrestrial spacecraft landed;
* claims that investigators physically entered or examined a craft;
* descriptions of communication with non-human intelligences;
* assertions of recovered technology or official cover-up operations;
* any claim that the Air Force had determined the nature of the phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

</div>

Even the observations it does include are presented cautiously. Descriptions such as objects that "appeared" to move, lights that "seemed" to behave unusually, or beams that were reportedly observed are recorded without explaining their cause. The memorandum preserves witness observations rather than converting them into established facts.

This distinction matters because later retellings often merge the contents of the memorandum with decades of additional testimony, making it appear that all later claims existed in the original official record. They did not.

## What the Memo Establishes as Historical Evidence

For historians and researchers, Halt's memorandum remains significant precisely because it is contemporaneous.

It establishes several propositions with considerable confidence:

* a senior USAF officer considered the reports credible enough to document officially;
* unusual lights were reported by multiple military personnel;
* Halt personally participated in a follow-up investigation;
* the incident entered British government files through official correspondence;
* the observations were regarded as unidentified at the time the report was written.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.nationalarchives.gov.uk/index.php/ufo-files-national-archives/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO files at The National Archives &#124; The National ArchivesMay 1, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2018</span></span></span>

Those are substantial historical facts irrespective of how the underlying events are interpreted.

By contrast, the memorandum alone cannot establish that an unidentified craft landed, that the observed lights represented advanced technology, or that the military confirmed an extraterrestrial presence. Those conclusions require additional evidence beyond what the document itself records.

## Reading the Memo in Context

Halt's memorandum carries unusual weight because it bridges eyewitness testimony and official record-keeping. Without it, the Rendlesham Forest incident would rely far more heavily on personal recollections. With it, there is documentary confirmation that senior military personnel formally reported unexplained observations.

Its importance therefore lies in documenting that something unusual was considered worthy of official reporting—not in proving what that something was.

When read carefully and without later embellishments, the memorandum supports a measured conclusion: it is strong evidence that unexplained observations were reported through official military channels, but it is not evidence that either the United States Air Force or the British Ministry of Defence officially concluded that a landed extraterrestrial craft had been found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>
