## Introduction

One of the easiest ways for a distant light to seem unexpectedly close is to view it while moving through dense woodland. During the Rendlesham Forest incident, observers were not standing in an open field with an unobstructed horizon. They were walking among closely spaced trunks and branches, often at night, with few reliable visual references. Under those conditions, a bright star or distant terrestrial light can appear to weave between trees, remain fixed within gaps in the canopy, or even seem to accompany the observer. This is not because the light itself is moving through the forest, but because the observer's motion continually changes the relationship between nearby foreground objects and a virtually stationary background source. Motion [parallax]({{ 'parallax/' | relative_url }}), combined with poor distance cues in darkness, provides a well-established perceptual mechanism for why remote lights can appear local and mobile.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/motion_parallax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lancaster.ac.uk">[lancaster.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lancaster.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lancaster University Motion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child Development</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lancaster UniversityMotion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child DevelopmentMay 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2019</span></span></span>

## How foreground motion alters apparent position

Motion parallax is a normal visual cue that helps people judge depth. As an observer moves, nearby objects sweep rapidly across the visual field, while distant objects appear to move much more slowly. Anyone looking sideways from a moving vehicle experiences this effect: roadside trees rush past, distant hills drift slowly, and the Moon seems almost motionless.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/motion_parallax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lancaster.ac.uk">[Lancaster University]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lancaster.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lancaster University Motion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child Development</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lancaster UniversityMotion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child DevelopmentMay 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2019</span></span></span>

A forest exaggerates this contrast because it is packed with nearby visual features. Every step causes trunks, branches and foliage to slide across the line of sight at different speeds. A distant point of light remains almost fixed in space, yet continually appears and disappears behind different trees. Instead of being recognised as an astronomical object far beyond the woodland, the light may seem to occupy successive openings between trunks.

The result is a compelling illusion that the light is navigating through the forest itself. The observer is actually moving relative to the trees, but the visual system can attribute the changing alignment to motion by the light instead. Aviation safety literature notes that parallax under low-light conditions can make fixed celestial objects appear nearby and in motion when observers themselves are moving through a dark environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_246.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asrs.arc.nasa.gov">[ASRS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asrs.arc.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span></span></span>

## Why walking observers lose reliable distance cues

In daylight, many independent cues reveal how far away an object is. Texture, colour, shadows, familiar object sizes and the visible horizon all contribute to distance estimation. At night inside woodland, many of these cues disappear.

Instead, the observer sees:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* isolated patches of sky separated by dark trunks;
* silhouettes with little detail or texture;
* a bright point of light with no visible surrounding landscape;
* constantly changing viewing angles while walking.

</div>

Because stars are effectively infinitely distant, they show almost no true positional change as someone walks a few metres. Nearby trees, however, move dramatically across the observer's field of view. Without an open horizon to reveal the light's true position, the brain can struggle to assign it an accurate distance. Rather than interpreting the light as extremely remote, it may instead place it somewhere among the visible trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4901450/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central (PMC)The neural basis of depth perception from motion parallax</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed Central (PMC)The neural basis of depth perception from motion parallax - PMC...</span></span></span>

This effect is particularly strong when attention is focused on a single bright light. Peripheral information that would normally anchor the scene is reduced, making it harder to distinguish genuine object motion from changes produced by the observer's own movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698900001498" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A slowly moving foreground can capture an observer’s self-motion — a report of a new motion illusion: inverted vection - Sci...</span></span></span>

## How changing tree gaps mimic pursuit or drift

A distinctive consequence of woodland parallax is the impression that a light is "following" the observer.

Imagine a bright star viewed through a narrow gap between two trunks. After a few steps, that gap closes while another opens slightly to one side. The same star immediately becomes visible through the new opening. To an observer concentrating on the light rather than on the shifting foreground, the light seems to have moved sideways through the trees.

Repeated dozens of times during a walk, this creates impressions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the light pacing the observer;
* the light weaving between trunks;
* the light stopping whenever the observer stops;
* the light drifting in response to the observer's own movement.

</div>

None of these perceptions requires the light itself to change position. They emerge naturally from the continuous rearrangement of foreground occlusions as the observer moves through dense woodland. Similar effects are recognised in aviation, where distant lights or planets can appear to manoeuvre because the aircraft—and therefore the observer—is moving while reliable visual references are scarce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_246.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asrs.arc.nasa.gov">[ASRS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asrs.arc.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span></span></span>

## Why the illusion becomes convincing in Rendlesham-like conditions

Several characteristics of Rendlesham Forest would have strengthened this mechanism.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://historical.parallax.kr/a/rendlesham-forest-incident-1980" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: historical.parallax.kr">[historical.parallax.kr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">historical.parallax.kr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">kr Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Historical Parallax</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forest Incident &#124; Historical ParallaxDecember 21, 2025 — Last updated: 2025-12-21 RENDLESHAM FOREST INCIDENT Image: Rendlesham Forest Inc...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 21, 2025</span></span></span>

The woodland consists of regularly spaced conifer trunks with relatively open lower sections but dense upper branches. At night these trunks become high-contrast vertical markers against small patches of sky. A bright celestial object viewed through them repeatedly appears, disappears and reappears as observers walk.

The situation is further complicated if the light is already exhibiting atmospheric [scintillation]({{ 'scintillation/' | relative_url }}). Rapid fluctuations in brightness and colour make the light seem active even while parallax makes its apparent position shift among the trees. The combined impression is of an object that is simultaneously flashing, changing colour and moving through the forest, despite the underlying source remaining fixed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://asrs.arc.nasa.gov/publications/callback/cb_246.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: asrs.arc.nasa.gov">[ASRS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">asrs.arc.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASRSCALLBACK 246</span></span></span>

Expectation can also amplify the experience. Once observers believe they are tracking an unusual object, attention naturally remains locked on the bright light rather than on the moving foreground. This selective attention makes the continual rearrangement of [tree gaps]({{ 'tree-gaps/' | relative_url }}) less noticeable while making the apparent motion of the light more compelling.

## What forest parallax can—and cannot—explain

Forest parallax provides a plausible explanation for reports involving a distant light that seemed to drift between trees, remain just ahead of observers, or appear unexpectedly close despite being stationary. It illustrates how sincere witnesses can perceive genuine motion without any physical movement by the light itself.

However, the mechanism has clear limits. It does not explain reports of structured objects seen at close range, physical traces, or observations made without intervening foreground features. Its explanatory value is specific to situations in which observers move through woodland while watching a bright, distant light under dark conditions.

Within the broader discussion of atmospheric scintillation and apparent UFO motion, forest parallax therefore acts as a complementary mechanism. Scintillation can make a fixed star appear to flash and change colour, while woodland parallax can simultaneously make that same distant light appear to shift among the trees and occupy the forest rather than the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fas/psych/glossary/motion_parallax/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lancaster.ac.uk">[lancaster.ac.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lancaster.ac.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lancaster University Motion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child Development</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lancaster UniversityMotion parallax – Lancaster Glossary of Child DevelopmentMay 22, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 22, 2019</span></span></span>

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
