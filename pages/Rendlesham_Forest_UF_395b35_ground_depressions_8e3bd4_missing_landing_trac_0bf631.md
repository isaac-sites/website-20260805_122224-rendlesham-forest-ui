## Introduction

If an aircraft or other physical machine had genuinely landed in the clearing associated with the Rendlesham Forest incident, investigators would normally expect more than three shallow depressions in the soil. The central forensic question is not whether ground marks existed—they did—but whether those marks displayed the range of physical effects that mechanical contact with the ground would ordinarily produce. Contemporary descriptions, later investigations and surviving documentation consistently show that no such diagnostic evidence was established. Instead, the recorded impressions remained isolated observations that were never convincingly linked to a heavy object or identifiable landing apparatus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## Expected Signs of Mechanical Ground Contact

When forensic investigators assess alleged landing sites, they look for a combination of mutually reinforcing physical traces rather than a single feature. Three shallow depressions alone are rarely sufficient because many natural and human activities can disturb soft woodland soil.

A genuine landing by a substantial machine would typically be expected to leave several categories of evidence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Consistent compression patterns.** Landing feet or skids should compress the soil beneath them in proportion to the object's weight. Investigators would expect measurable deformation extending below the surface rather than isolated scoops or irregular scrapes.
* **Matching contact geometry.** If three landing supports produced the impressions, their size, shape, spacing and orientation should display consistent engineering rather than approximate similarity.
* **Displaced soil.** Mechanical loading often pushes soil sideways, creating characteristic rims, shear fractures or compacted edges that distinguish compression from excavation by animals or erosion.
* **Transferred material.** Metal particles, paint fragments, lubricants, synthetic residues or unusual mineral contamination can sometimes be transferred between machinery and soil during contact.
* **Vegetation damage.** Moss, needles, roots and surrounding plants would normally show crushing, tearing or prolonged disturbance if a heavy object rested on them.
* **Associated disturbance.** Nearby branches, debris and ground cover might show consistent physical interaction rather than isolated incidental damage.

</div>

No published examination demonstrated this combination of features at the Rendlesham site. Contemporary reports describe the depressions, but they do not establish that they possessed characteristics uniquely associated with a landed vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>

## Tests That Were Never Completed

The evidential value of the depressions was limited not only by what was found but also by what was never systematically examined.

A modern forensic investigation would ideally document the site through precise surveying, detailed photography, three-dimensional measurement and controlled sampling before the area became contaminated. Such work could have addressed several important questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Were the depressions identical in profile or merely similar in appearance?
* Did the soil beneath them show measurable compaction compared with surrounding ground?
* Were microscopic foreign materials present?
* Did the impressions penetrate undisturbed soil horizons, indicating recent formation?
* Could their spacing support a mechanically plausible landing geometry?

</div>

Although plaster casts were reportedly made, no widely published technical analysis demonstrated that the casts revealed manufactured shapes or engineering features. Likewise, no detailed geotechnical report established abnormal soil compression or subsurface damage beneath the impressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span></span></span>

The absence of documented laboratory examination means the marks cannot be positively identified as either landing gear impressions or ordinary woodland disturbances solely from the available record.

## The Problem of Contamination

One of the few physical measurements discussed after the incident involved soil composition. However, this illustrates another forensic difficulty.

Later discussion noted the presence of calcium carbonate in samples from the site. Rather than indicating an exotic substance, investigators concluded that this was consistent with contamination introduced when plaster casts were made of the depressions. Once casting material enters the soil, distinguishing original chemical traces from investigative contamination becomes much more difficult.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wiki Disc Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; Wiki Disc</span></span></span>

This highlights a broader principle in forensic science: evidence collected after a scene has been disturbed often loses much of its diagnostic value unless contamination is carefully controlled and documented.

## Why Missing Traces Weaken the Landing Claim

The strongest physical case for a landed craft would not rest on the existence of three depressions alone. It would rely on multiple independent lines of evidence converging on the same conclusion.

Instead, investigators found important gaps:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no demonstrated engineered footprint pattern;
* no verified transfer of unusual material;
* no published evidence of significant soil compaction beneath the marks;
* no documented subsurface deformation consistent with heavy loading;
* no reproducible laboratory findings distinguishing the site from ordinary forest ground.

</div>

At the same time, alternative explanations remained plausible. Suffolk police reportedly regarded the impressions as insignificant and compatible with animal activity, while Forestry Commission forester Vince Thurkettle later stated that the marks resembled rabbit diggings already present beneath accumulated pine needles. Whether or not those explanations are ultimately correct, they illustrate that the observed depressions lacked uniquely mechanical characteristics capable of excluding ordinary causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span></span></span>

## What the Physical Record Ultimately Supports

The surviving evidence supports a narrow conclusion. There were shallow ground disturbances at the reported location, and those disturbances became central to later claims of a landing. However, the forensic record never established the additional physical signatures normally expected if a solid machine had rested on the forest floor.

That distinction is significant. The existence of ground marks is an observable fact documented in official accounts. Interpreting those marks as the footprint of an unidentified craft requires evidence—consistent mechanical profiles, measurable compression, transferred material or other diagnostic traces—that was never conclusively demonstrated. As a result, the depressions remain suggestive but not forensically persuasive evidence for the landing claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/appendix.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian Ridpath Appendix – Col Halt&#x27;s memo to the Mo D</span></span></span>
