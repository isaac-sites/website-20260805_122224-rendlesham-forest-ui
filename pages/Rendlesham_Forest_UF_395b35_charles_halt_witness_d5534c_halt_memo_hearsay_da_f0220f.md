## Introduction

Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s memorandum of 13 January 1981 is one of the most important contemporary documents connected with the Rendlesham Forest incident, but it is not a flawless chronological record. Two distinct issues complicate its use as evidence. First, part of the memorandum summarises events that Halt did not personally witness, relying instead on reports from security personnel involved in the [first night]({{ 'first-night/' | relative_url }})'s investigation. Second, the memorandum contains date errors that shift the sequence of events by roughly one day. Neither issue necessarily invalidates the document, but both require historians and investigators to distinguish carefully between firsthand observations and secondhand reporting when reconstructing what happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/ufo-transcript-aug-09.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Script for National Archives UFO files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesScript for National Archives UFO files - August 2009 podcastMay 19, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 19, 2026</span></span></span>

## Which first-night claims Halt did not witness

Halt was not present during the initial encounter in the forest. The first reported sighting occurred during the early hours of 26 December 1980, when security police investigating what they believed might have been a crashed aircraft entered Rendlesham Forest. Halt only became directly involved during the later investigation, when he led a team into the forest during the early hours of 28 December.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial because the opening section of his memorandum describes events that had been relayed to him by other servicemen rather than personally observed. Among the claims included in the memorandum but not witnessed by Halt himself are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Reports that a glowing object with a metallic appearance stood in the forest.
* Descriptions of coloured lights on the object.
* Statements that the object manoeuvred through the trees as personnel attempted to approach it.
* Accounts that nearby farm animals became agitated during the encounter.

</div>

These descriptions have often been quoted as though they represented Halt's own observations. In reality, they summarise what subordinate personnel told him after the first night's events. The memorandum itself does not explicitly separate reported information from firsthand observations, which has contributed to later misunderstandings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendlesham_Forest_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rendlesham Forest incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rendlesham Forest incident</span></span></span>

By contrast, the later portions of the memorandum—covering the ground inspection, radiation measurements, observation of flashing lights, and aerial objects seen during Halt's own expedition—are based on his direct participation. This distinction between reported and observed material is fundamental when assessing the evidential value of individual claims.

## The memorandum's disputed dates

The best-known factual problem in the memorandum concerns its chronology.

The memorandum states that the first incident occurred on **27 December 1980** and that Halt's own investigation took place on **29 December 1980**. Contemporary [police records]({{ 'police-record/' | relative_url }}), witness timelines and subsequent historical reconstructions instead place those events during the early hours of **26 December** and **28 December**, respectively. In effect, both principal dates in the memorandum are shifted forward by approximately one day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[thecoldfile.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold File...</span></span></span>

Researchers from very different perspectives—including sceptics, government archivists and many proponents of the case—generally agree that these are genuine dating errors rather than evidence of additional unrecognised nights of activity. The corrected chronology has become the standard framework used in modern discussions of the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thecoldfile.com/articles/1980-rendlesham-forest/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thecoldfile.com">[thecoldfile.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thecoldfile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1980 rendlesham forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Cold FileLights in the Pines: The 1980 Rendlesham Forest Incident &#124; The Cold File...</span></span></span>

Halt has repeatedly acknowledged that the dates may have been mistaken. He explained that he drafted the memorandum roughly two weeks after the events from memory, later attempting unsuccessfully to verify the dates using security and police logs that were no longer available to him. He also indicated that he regarded the memorandum as a brief notification rather than a detailed investigative report, making the narrative more important to him than precise calendar dates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/the-halt-memorandum-1983.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the halt memorandum 1983</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest IncidentThe ‘Halt memorandum’ (1983)...</span></span></span>

The dating problem had practical consequences beyond later historical debate. Ministry of Defence staff and later researchers attempting to compare the memorandum with police records, [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}) information and astronomical data initially found the chronology confusing because the official memorandum did not align with independently documented dates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skeptic.org.uk/2006/04/from-the-archive-rendlesham-forest-britains-roswell/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptic.org.uk">[The Skeptic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptic.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Skeptic From the archive: Rendlesham Forest</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The SkepticFrom the archive: Rendlesham Forest - Britain’s Roswell? - The Skeptic...</span></span></span>

## How the errors affect the memorandum's evidential weight

The memorandum remains an important historical document, but its evidential value depends on how it is used.

For documenting that unusual reports reached a senior United States Air Force officer shortly after the events, the memorandum is highly significant. ## Why source separation matters

The memorandum illustrates a broader principle of historical analysis: official documents are not automatically identical to eyewitness testimony.

Halt occupied a dual role as both investigator and reporting officer. His memorandum preserved valuable information, but it also merged direct observation with summaries of reports received from subordinates. Once the dating errors became recognised, researchers increasingly treated the document as a hybrid source rather than a simple eyewitness narrative.

Accordingly, the strongest use of the memorandum is to distinguish between:

* events Halt personally experienced during the second investigation;
* events he recorded because other servicemen reported them to him; and
* administrative details, such as dates, that can be checked against independent [contemporary records]({{ 'contemporary-record/' | relative_url }}).

Making these distinctions allows the memorandum to remain a valuable primary source while avoiding the common mistake of treating it as a complete, error-free chronology of the Rendlesham Forest incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk/documents/ufo-transcript-aug-09.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Script for National Archives UFO files</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesScript for National Archives UFO files - August 2009 podcastMay 19, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 19, 2026</span></span></span>
