## Introduction

One of the recurring claims in the Rendlesham Forest UFO incident is that investigators found "burn marks" or unusual damage on pine trees near the alleged landing site. A more conventional explanation, advanced soon after the incident and repeated by later investigators, is that many of these marks were ordinary forestry axe blazes—cuts deliberately made into tree bark during woodland management. If correct, this interpretation would significantly weaken the argument that the tree damage represented physical evidence of an extraordinary event.

The available evidence does not conclusively prove that every marked tree seen by Lieutenant Colonel [Charles Halt]({{ 'charles-halt/' | relative_url }})'s team had been prepared for felling. However, it does show that managed pine plantations commonly contain axe blazes that expose resin, producing scars that can resemble burns or impact abrasions, particularly when examined at night under torchlight. Just as importantly, no surviving forensic comparison was ever conducted between the allegedly damaged trees and similar marked trees elsewhere in the plantation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## Were the "Burn Marks" Just Forestry Cuts?

The forestry explanation emerged because Rendlesham Forest was not an untouched woodland but a commercially managed Forestry Commission plantation. Trees were routinely inspected, thinned and marked during timber management.

According to investigator and science writer Ian Ridpath, who revisited the site in 1983 and interviewed local forestry worker Vince Thurkettle, the supposed "burn marks" were simply axe cuts made by foresters to identify trees intended for later felling. Ridpath photographed numerous examples in which fresh pine resin had oozed from the cuts, giving them a scorched or blistered appearance remarkably similar to later descriptions of anomalous damage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

This explanation gained additional support because the marks were not isolated. Comparable blazes could reportedly be found throughout the plantation rather than being confined to the alleged landing area. If true, that greatly reduces their evidential value as unique traces left by an unidentified object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## What Axe Blazes Look Like on Pine Trees

An axe blaze is created by chopping away a section of bark to expose the underlying wood. In commercial forestry these marks have long served practical purposes, including identifying trees for thinning, surveying or eventual harvesting.

On pine species, several features can make such cuts appear unusual:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Resin quickly flows from the wound**, creating glossy or crystalline deposits.
* **The exposed wood contrasts sharply with surrounding bark**, especially when illuminated by torchlight.
* **Weathering changes their appearance**, so some cuts appear fresh while others develop darker, rougher surfaces.
* **The wound remains visible for years**, because pines compartmentalise damage slowly rather than rapidly covering it with new bark.

</div>

These characteristics closely resemble parts of the observations recorded during Halt's investigation. On the audio recording, he described a roughly circular abrasion and commented that "it looks like it might be old", while also expressing surprise at the visible pine sap. Rather than confidently describing recent burning, he repeatedly qualified his observations with uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/the-halt-tape-colonel-halts-audio-tape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest IncidentThe Halt Tape (Colonel Halt&#x27;s Audio Tape Recording) -&quot;Now were observing what appears to be a beam coming d...</span></span></span>

## How the Forestry Explanation Fits the Halt Tape

The Halt tape is particularly important because it captures observations in real time, before memories could evolve.

Several aspects of the recording fit comfortably with the forestry-cut interpretation.

First, Halt himself questioned whether at least one abrasion was old rather than freshly created. That hesitation is significant because it shows he did not immediately interpret the marks as recent damage caused by an unidentified craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/the-halt-tape-colonel-halts-audio-tape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest IncidentThe Halt Tape (Colonel Halt&#x27;s Audio Tape Recording) -&quot;Now were observing what appears to be a beam coming d...</span></span></span>

Second, the team discussed collecting a bark sample that included the visible resin. The tape records interest in the sap itself but does not identify any unusual chemical property, scorching or charring. No publicly available laboratory report has ever demonstrated that the sample contained anything inconsistent with ordinary pine resin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.therendleshamforestincident.com/2022/04/the-halt-tape-colonel-halts-audio-tape.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: therendleshamforestincident.com">[The Rendlesham Forest Incident]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">therendleshamforestincident.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Rendlesham Forest IncidentThe Halt Tape (Colonel Halt&#x27;s Audio Tape Recording) -&quot;Now were observing what appears to be a beam coming d...</span></span></span>

Third, although Lieutenant Bruce Englund remarked that several abrasions appeared to face towards the centre of the clearing, this observation was never followed by systematic measurements. ## Why the Comparison Remains Incomplete

Although the forestry explanation is plausible, it is not a complete demonstration that every observed mark was an axe blaze.

Several limitations remain.

**No contemporary forestry records have been published** identifying the precise trees examined by Halt's team or confirming that each had been officially marked for harvesting.

**No controlled photographic survey exists.** Ideally, investigators would have photographed the alleged burn marks alongside ordinary axe blazes elsewhere in Rendlesham Forest under identical lighting conditions. Such a comparison was never undertaken publicly.

**Some witness descriptions vary.** Later retellings sometimes describe bark damage in broader or more dramatic terms than those preserved on the original tape, making it difficult to distinguish between contemporaneous observations and later memory.

These gaps mean the forestry explanation is supported by analogy rather than by a documented tree-by-tree identification. That distinction is important because it avoids replacing one unsupported certainty with another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>

## What This Means for the Physical Evidence

When considered alongside the broader physical record, the axe-blaze hypothesis substantially reduces the evidential weight of the alleged tree damage.

If ordinary forestry cuts can account for the observed abrasions, then the marks cease to be independent evidence of an object striking or heating the trees. Instead, they become an example of how familiar features in a managed forest may acquire extraordinary significance when viewed after reports of an unusual event.

This does not explain every aspect of the Rendlesham incident, nor does it address reports of lights or witness perceptions. It is a narrower point about the physical traces themselves. The strongest contemporary documentation shows investigators observing abrasions, resin and uncertainty—not verified scorch marks, burned bark or impact damage established through forensic examination. In that respect, the forestry explanation remains one of the most credible conventional accounts for the [tree marks]({{ 'trunk-marks/' | relative_url }}), while also highlighting a larger weakness in the case: the absence of systematic comparison with ordinary features already present in the forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham1a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[ianridpath.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original article</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO explained – the original articleJanuary 5, 1985...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 5, 1985</span></span></span>
