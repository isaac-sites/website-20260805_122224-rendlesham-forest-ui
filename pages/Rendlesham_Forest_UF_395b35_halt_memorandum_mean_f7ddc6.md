## Introduction

The Halt memorandum confirms that United States Air Force personnel reported unusual lights and possible ground traces near RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }}) in December 1980. It does **not** confirm that an extraterrestrial vehicle landed, that a structured craft was detected by [radar]({{ 'radar/' | relative_url }}), or that the British government accepted the witnesses’ interpretation.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-overview"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Rendlesham_Forest_UF_395b35_halt_memorandum_mean_f7ddc6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Halt Memo" width="1536" height="1024" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Illustrative overview</span></figcaption></figure>

Dated 13 January 1981 and headed **“[Unexplained Lights]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }})”**, the one-page memorandum was written by Lieutenant Colonel Charles I. Halt, deputy base commander at Woodbridge, and passed through the local Royal Air Force liaison channel to the Ministry of Defence. It became the Rendlesham Forest incident’s most important official document because it placed striking witness claims on military stationery and brought them into a government file. Yet its official meaning was narrower: it was a report of observations requiring assessment, not a finding about what caused them. The Ministry of Defence subsequently judged that the information disclosed nothing of defence significance and did not open a full investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalarchives.gov.uk">[nationalarchives.gov.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalarchives.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives UFO reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesUFO reports - The National Archives## Correspondence on the Rendlesham Forest incident... ## Why the memorandum was written

The reported events occurred outside the Woodbridge perimeter, in British territory, at a time when the United States Air Force no longer operated a formal UFO-investigation programme comparable to Project Blue Book. The practical solution was therefore to pass a written account to the British authorities responsible for the surrounding territory and national air defence. ## What Halt recorded

The memorandum divides naturally into two episodes. The first paragraph recounts what security personnel reported during the initial encounter. The later paragraphs describe the inspection of the alleged site and the lights observed while Halt accompanied an investigative party.

For the first episode, Halt wrote that two security policemen saw unusual lights outside the rear gate at Woodbridge at approximately 3am. Believing that an aircraft might have crashed or made a forced landing, personnel entered the forest to investigate. The memorandum says that they reported a glowing object with a metallic appearance, roughly triangular in shape, illuminating the forest with a white light and carrying red and blue lights. It was said to have manoeuvred through the trees and then disappeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHalt_Memorandum.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Halt Memorandum.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Early in the morning of 27 Dec 80 (approximately 0300L), two USAF... night (29 Dec 80) the area was checked for radiation...</span></span></span>

That wording is significant, but its source must be kept clear. Halt did not witness the reported triangular object described in the first paragraph. He was summarising what other airmen had told him. The paragraph is therefore an official record of their claim, not independent corroboration by the memorandum’s author.

The document next records an examination of the area described as the landing site. Halt wrote that three depressions were found in the ground, arranged in a roughly triangular pattern, and that a radiation detector produced readings around the depressions and on a nearby tree. These passages explain why the memorandum remains compelling to many readers. It preserves extraordinary descriptions attributed to military personnel and includes claims made by a senior officer who participated in the later observation. What it preserves, however, are descriptions: apparent shape, apparent movement, coloured lights and perceived beams. It supplies no photograph, recovered material, radar plot, calibrated environmental survey or other instrument record capable of identifying the source.

## What “unexplained” officially meant

The title **“Unexplained Lights”** is often treated as if “unexplained” meant “inexplicable by ordinary causes” or “confirmed unknown technology”. In administrative usage, it meant only that the report did not contain an established identification when submitted.

An observation may remain unexplained because the available information is insufficient, because no detailed investigation is undertaken, or because several possible explanations cannot be separated retrospectively. None of those situations establishes an extraordinary origin. “Unexplained” describes the state of the file, not the nature of the object.

The memorandum itself contains no conclusion that the lights came from another planet, that the supposed object was an aircraft of unknown manufacture, or even that every description concerned the same physical source. It does not use the word “extraterrestrial”. Nor does it claim that an alien craft landed. Its strongest language remains observational: witnesses saw lights, reported an object that appeared metallic, found depressions and obtained detector readings.

The distinction is particularly important because later retellings frequently convert reported appearances into established facts. A light that “appeared” to move becomes a manoeuvring vehicle; marks found in the ground become landing-gear impressions; a detector reading becomes residual radiation from a craft. Those transformations go beyond what the document demonstrates.

The memorandum is strongest as evidence that the reports existed by January 1981 and were taken seriously enough locally to be written down. It is much weaker as evidence that the witnesses’ interpretation was physically correct.

## How the Ministry of Defence assessed it

The Ministry of Defence treated the memorandum primarily as an air-defence question: did the reported activity indicate an unauthorised aircraft, hostile reconnaissance, a penetration of British airspace or another event with military significance?

Parliament was later told that staff responsible for air-defence matters assessed the report and judged that it contained nothing of defence significance. No formal reply was sent to Halt and no further action was taken. The Ministry also stated that it had no record of an official assessment comparing the reported [radiation readings]({{ 'radiation/' | relative_url }}) with normal background radiation in Rendlesham Forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK Parliament...</span></span></span>

The absence of radar confirmation does not prove that nobody saw unusual lights. Many distant, low-altitude, faint or non-airborne light sources would not appear on military radar. ## What the memorandum does not establish

The official status of the document makes it authentic evidence, but authenticity must not be confused with proof of every claim it contains. The memorandum does not establish several points often attributed to it:

* **It does not certify an alien landing.** It reports lights and an object as described by witnesses but offers no conclusion about extraterrestrial origin.
* **It does not provide direct testimony from every participant.** Its first paragraph is Halt’s summary of what other servicemen reportedly experienced.
* **It does not verify that the [ground depressions]({{ 'ground-marks/' | relative_url }}) were made by a vehicle.** It records their presence and arrangement without demonstrating their cause.
* **It does not prove abnormal radiation.** Measurements were listed without a proper baseline or official scientific evaluation; the Ministry later said it held no record of such an assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK Parliament...</span></span></span>
* **It does not establish radar confirmation.** The released official record instead reports that no relevant unexplained track was detected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drdavidclarke.co.uk/rendlesham-forest-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drdavidclarke.co.uk">[Dr. David Clarke]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drdavidclarke.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. David Clarke Rendlesham Forest UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dr. David Clarke Rendlesham Forest UFOs</span></span></span>
* **It does not contain the later, more elaborate elements of the Rendlesham story.** Claims involving extensive examination of symbols, telepathic messages, binary code or confirmed extraterrestrial occupants are not findings of the January 1981 memorandum.

Its omissions are equally revealing. There are no witness names in the narrative, no attached statements, no map, no instrument specifications, no weather information, no astronomical comparison and no account of attempts to identify terrestrial lights. It is a concise notification, not a complete evidential dossier.

## The document’s real significance

The Halt memorandum remains central because it fixes a limited but important core of the Rendlesham story in the contemporary record. By 13 January 1981, a deputy base commander had formally reported that military personnel saw unusual lights, investigated a possible landing site and remained unable to identify everything they observed. That fact cannot reasonably be dismissed as a story invented decades later.

At the same time, the document places firm boundaries around the claim. It demonstrates that unusual experiences were reported; it does not demonstrate what produced them. Its date errors, mixture of first-hand and second-hand material, lack of supporting attachments and absence of instrumentally confirmed air activity all restrict the conclusions that can be drawn from it.

The Ministry of Defence’s judgement was similarly limited. “No defence significance” did not mean that officials had solved every light or proved that all witnesses were mistaken. It meant that the available information did not indicate an airspace violation or military threat requiring further official action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hansard.parliament.uk/lords/1997-10-14/debates/96394ce5-f0db-4448-af50-43a69fbf3c57/LieutenantColonelCharlesHaltMemorandum" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hansard.parliament.uk">[Hansard]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hansard.parliament.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Hansard Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lieutenant Colonel Charles Halt: Memorandum - Hansard - UK Parliament...</span></span></span>

The most accurate reading therefore lies between two common extremes. The memorandum is not government confirmation of an extraterrestrial craft, but neither is it a fabricated or meaningless scrap of paper. It is an authentic military report documenting unresolved observations whose extraordinary interpretation remained unverified.
