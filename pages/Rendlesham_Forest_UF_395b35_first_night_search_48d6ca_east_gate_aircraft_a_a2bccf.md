## Introduction

The first response to the Rendlesham Forest incident was driven by routine military procedure rather than any expectation of encountering an unidentified craft. In the early hours of 26 December 1980, security personnel at RAF [Woodbridge]({{ 'woodbridge/' | relative_url }}) noticed coloured lights beyond the East Gate, just outside the airfield perimeter. Given the location beside an operational military runway, the most immediate assumption was that an aircraft had crashed, made a forced landing or caught fire in the forest. That assessment shaped every decision made during the opening phase of the incident: security police were dispatched as an emergency search party, commanders coordinated their movements by radio, and local police were informed while the search was still underway. Contemporary documents consistently show that the operation began as an attempt to locate a possible aviation emergency, only later becoming associated with the famous UFO controversy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

## The first lights seen beyond the perimeter

The initial observations came from the eastern side of RAF Woodbridge, where the base bordered Rendlesham Forest. Security personnel reported unusual coloured lights beyond the perimeter fence, including red, blue and white lights that appeared low among the trees rather than high overhead. From the East Gate, the lights seemed close enough to suggest an incident within or immediately beyond the woodland.

This location mattered operationally. The forest lay directly outside a secure military installation, and any [unexplained]({{ 'unexplained/' | relative_url }}) illumination could indicate an aircraft accident, an unauthorised landing, a fire or a security breach. None of these possibilities could safely be ignored by an airbase responsible for military aircraft and sensitive facilities. As a result, the report was treated as an operational matter requiring immediate investigation rather than as an unexplained aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

Later reconstructions of the witnesses' original statements show that personnel initially described lights rather than a clearly identifiable object. The apparent source also became increasingly difficult to judge as investigators moved towards it, with the lights seeming to shift position or recede deeper into the forest. This growing uncertainty developed only after the search had already begun.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

## Why an aircraft accident seemed plausible

From the perspective of the security police, assuming an aircraft emergency was entirely reasonable.

Several practical considerations supported that conclusion:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The lights appeared beyond the airfield perimeter close to an active runway.
* Witnesses believed they had seen something descending or stationary near the ground.
* Military personnel were trained to respond immediately to reports of aircraft crashes or forced landings.
* A delay could have endangered aircrew if an aircraft had indeed come down in woodland.

</div>

Lieutenant Fred "Skip" Buran, the senior security officer overseeing the shift, later explained that he authorised the investigation because the available information suggested the possibility of a crashed aircraft. His decision reflected standard emergency procedure rather than an interest in unidentified flying objects. When no wreckage was immediately located, he later ordered the patrol to return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

This operational mindset also explains why the search was conducted by security police rather than by investigators specialising in unusual phenomena. Their immediate task was straightforward: locate a possible crash site, assist survivors if necessary and secure the area.

## How the search team was assembled

Once the report reached security control, personnel organised a small search party rather than a large-scale emergency deployment.

Staff Sergeant Jim Penniston, one of the senior security policemen on duty, became the principal investigator in the field. Airman First Class John [Burroughs]({{ 'burroughs/' | relative_url }}) accompanied him, while Airman Edward [Cabansag]({{ 'cabansag/' | relative_url }}) also took part in the initial response. Other personnel remained at command posts or acted as radio relays between the search team and Central Security Control, allowing commanders to monitor developments without sending excessive numbers of personnel into the forest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

The limited size of the team reflected the information available at the time. Witnesses had reported unusual lights, not a confirmed air disaster. A small patrol could rapidly assess the situation while avoiding unnecessary disruption to base operations.

As the patrol advanced through the trees, however, the apparent source of the lights became increasingly difficult to locate. Instead of finding wreckage, fire or injured aircrew, they encountered shifting points of light whose distance proved hard to estimate. That transition—from an expected rescue or security operation to an increasingly uncertain pursuit—marked the point at which the incident diverged from an ordinary emergency response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

## The East Gate alarm became a documented police matter

One of the strongest pieces of contemporary evidence for the nature of the initial response comes from Suffolk Constabulary records.

At approximately 4:11 a.m. on 26 December 1980, RAF Woodbridge's law-enforcement desk contacted Suffolk Police to report unusual lights and advised that unarmed personnel had been sent to investigate. Police officers checked with air traffic control and searched the surrounding area but found no evidence of an aircraft accident. The only continuing light they were able to identify was the beam from Orfordness [Lighthouse]({{ 'lighthouse/' | relative_url }}), visible from parts of the search area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ianridpath.com/ufo/rendlesham2c.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ianridpath.com">[Ian Ridpath]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ianridpath.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ian Ridpath Rendlesham Forest UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ian RidpathRendlesham Forest UFO - The witness statements 2...</span></span></span>

This [police record]({{ 'police-record/' | relative_url }}) is significant because it captures the incident while events were still unfolding.
